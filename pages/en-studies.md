# Research programme

[PL](studies.md) · [EN](en-studies.md)

## Creators' experiences

Individual interviews will examine motives for creating music, the circumstances in which tracks are produced and the meanings participants attribute to music in relation to their personal experiences.

## Emotion regulation

A survey will investigate associations between the use of music generators, alexithymia, emotion regulation, rumination and well-being. The proposed AIM-ER (AI-assisted Music for Emotion Regulation) model provides a theoretical basis for hypotheses about the mechanisms underlying these associations.

## Agency and authorship

Further studies will examine the role of personal contribution to music creation and perceived control over the finished work. We are also considering diary studies to examine emotional changes over time.

<p class="study-status">Study protocols are currently being developed.</p>

<article class="pub" id="msc-mus-p01" aria-labelledby="msc-mus-p01-title">
<div class="pub-meta"><p class="pub-id">MSC-MUS-P01</p><p class="pub-type">Theoretical paper</p></div>
<h3 id="msc-mus-p01-title">AIM-ER (AI-assisted Music for Emotion Regulation) model of emotion regulation</h3>
<p class="pub-question"><strong>Research question:</strong> Does emotion regulation depend on the fit between the creator’s goal and the affordances of the music generator?</p>
<div class="pub-body"><div><h4>Scope and methodology</h4><p>The model separates regulatory goals, creative operations (prompting, generation, selection and editing), psychological mechanisms, and immediate and delayed outcomes. Ten falsifiable hypotheses address distress reduction, personal resonance, authorship, agency, alexithymia, rumination, feedback, musical expertise, regulatory flexibility and novelty. A central prediction distinct from H1–H10 is tested in a proposed 2 × 2 experiment crossing goal (emotional articulation or reinterpretation) with interface affordance (preserving meaning or transforming perspective), with comparable duration and generator access.</p></div><div><h4>Intended scientific contribution</h4><p>The primary experimental outcome is goal attainment, with emotional clarity and reinterpretation assessed separately. The framework predicts an advantage for matched conditions, not universal AI effectiveness. A precise null or reversed effect would weaken the prediction. The research programme includes interviews, surveys, diary studies and experiments, separating immediate relief from later functioning and proposed mechanisms from causal evidence.</p></div></div>
<p class="pub-target"><strong>Status:</strong> Developed theoretical manuscript · <strong>Target journal:</strong> <i>Musicae Scientiae</i></p>
</article>
<article class="pub" id="msc-mus-p02" aria-labelledby="msc-mus-p02-title">
<div class="pub-meta"><p class="pub-id">MSC-MUS-P02</p><p class="pub-type">Qualitative study</p></div>
<h3 id="msc-mus-p02-title">From personal experience to music</h3>
<p class="pub-question"><strong>Research question:</strong> How do creators describe transforming personal experience into music, including unsuccessful and emotionally neutral attempts?</p>
<div class="pub-body"><div><h4>Scope and methodology</h4><p>The protocol proposes purposive recruitment of approximately 20–30 Polish-speaking adult generator users with varied musical experience. Individual online semi-structured interviews reconstruct the most recent eligible creative episode, including motives, prompting, selection and rejection of versions, perceived agency and personal resonance. Participants may discuss an optional contrasting episode and voluntarily share an artefact; intimate disclosure is not required. Contextualist reflexive thematic analysis considers divergent experiences, using AIM-ER (AI-assisted Music for Emotion Regulation) as sensitising concepts rather than a compulsory coding scheme.</p></div><div><h4>Intended scientific contribution</h4><p>The study aims to distinguish repeated generation for aesthetic exploration from repetition associated with emotional engagement or frustration. Including accounts without emotional benefits avoids focusing solely on successful tracks. Themes will inform survey wording and the future AI-MRFS instrument, but cannot establish prevalence, clinical effectiveness or causal emotion regulation.</p></div></div>
<p class="pub-target"><strong>Status:</strong> Qualitative protocol, before data collection · <strong>Target journal:</strong> <i>Psychology of Music</i></p>
</article>
<article class="pub" id="msc-mus-p03" aria-labelledby="msc-mus-p03-title">
<div class="pub-meta"><p class="pub-id">MSC-MUS-P03</p><p class="pub-type">Survey study</p></div>
<h3 id="msc-mus-p03-title">Motives and functions of music creation</h3>
<p class="pub-question"><strong>Research question:</strong> Is a stronger initial emotional-expression motive associated with greater retrospective post-episode emotional clarity?</p>
<div class="pub-body"><div><h4>Scope and methodology</h4><p>A cross-sectional online survey will recruit Polish-speaking adults. Each reconstructs the most recent episode within 30 days, including unfinished or abandoned attempts. Four motives (expression, mood improvement, exploration and entertainment) and recalled pre/post clarity and valence are rated on 0–10 scales. The proposed target is 480 complete eligible primary records, with a cap of 560 submissions or eight weeks. OLS with HC3 uncertainty adjusts for initial clarity, competing motives, age, musical experience, use frequency, episode recency and recruitment source. Secondary hypotheses concern valence, iterative operations (0–3), and agency in relation to personal resonance, with Holm correction.</p></div><div><h4>Intended scientific contribution</h4><p>The analysis separates initial motivation, creative operations and perceived consequences. A total of 40,000 synthetic replications examined dependence among motives, rounded ratings and missingness. In one outcome-dependent missingness scenario, rejection at the alternative fell to 39.65%, motivating sensitivity analysis. These are synthetic data, not survey findings. Retrospective ratings cannot establish a causal effect of creation.</p></div></div>
<p class="pub-target"><strong>Status:</strong> Developed survey protocol, before data collection · <strong>Target journal:</strong> <i>Psychology of Music</i></p>
</article>
<article class="pub" id="msc-mus-p04" aria-labelledby="msc-mus-p04-title">
<div class="pub-meta"><p class="pub-id">MSC-MUS-P04</p><p class="pub-type">Randomised online experiment</p></div>
<h3 id="msc-mus-p04-title">Alexithymia and emotional expression</h3>
<p class="pub-question"><strong>Research question:</strong> Does overall alexithymia modify the effect of access to AI emotion-language suggestions on expressive success?</p>
<div class="pub-body"><div><h4>Scope and methodology</h4><p>A planned 1:1 randomised experiment compares eight minutes of assembling a sketch from identical pre-generated instrumental clips, with or without emotion-language suggestions. The moderator is the Polish PAQ-S (six items, total 6–42; no separate facets). The primary score is fit to intended emotion on a 0–10 scale; confirmed absence of a meaningful sketch is zero, while unknown status or missing applicable rating is missing. Emotional clarity is measured before the task and after suggestions are removed, with an optional 24-hour assessment. H1 uses the assignment-by-PAQ-S interaction, HC3 uncertainty, 50 PMM imputations and 27 missingness sensitivity scenarios.</p></div><div><h4>Intended scientific contribution</h4><p>The study separates assisted expression from unaided emotional understanding. A 30–50-person pilot is proposed; 1,000 randomised participants is a planning ceiling, not a fixed sample size. Calibration of 720 synthetic datasets (50 imputations per method) and 3,600 measurement-error datasets highlighted vulnerability to outcome-dependent missingness. These are not participant results. The experiment tests access to a scaffold in this task, not superiority of AI-authored over human-authored language or therapeutic efficacy.</p></div></div>
<p class="pub-target"><strong>Status:</strong> Developed experimental protocol, before data collection · <strong>Target journal:</strong> <i>Psychology of Music</i></p>
</article>

<h3 id="msc-mus-p05-title">Measuring regulation functions: AI-MRFS</h3>
<p class="pub-question"><strong>Research question:</strong> How can the distinct regulation functions of AI-assisted music creation be measured reliably?</p>
<div class="pub-body"><div><h4>Scope and methodology</h4><p>Candidate items will draw on qualitative findings and AIM-ER (AI-assisted Music for Emotion Regulation). Planned work includes expert and user content review, cognitive interviews, factor-structure analysis, reliability with uncertainty intervals, and convergent and discriminant validity. Structural validation requires a separate sample.</p></div><div><h4>Intended scientific contribution</h4><p>A measure distinguishing different creative functions instead of assuming a single overall score. AI-MRFS is under development, not a validated scale.</p></div></div>

<h3 id="msc-mus-p06-title">Rumination and avoidance</h3>
<p class="pub-question"><strong>Research question:</strong> When might repeated emotional themes accompany constructive reflection, and when might they accompany perseveration or avoidance?</p>
<div class="pub-body"><div><h4>Scope and methodology</h4><p>Planned measures of rumination, avoidance, subjective relief and everyday functioning will be analysed in relation to motives and creative context. Separating immediate and delayed relief will help examine distinct patterns; observational findings will concern associations rather than causes.</p></div><div><h4>Intended scientific contribution</h4><p>Identification of potentially adaptive and maladaptive functions without inferring harm from the emotional content of music alone.</p></div></div>

<h3 id="msc-mus-p07-title">Emotional changes in everyday life</h3>
<p class="pub-question"><strong>Research question:</strong> How does affect change before a creative session, immediately afterward and at a later assessment?</p>
<div class="pub-body"><div><h4>Scope and methodology</h4><p>Repeated naturalistic measurements will cover mood, regulatory goal, session characteristics, rumination and delayed functioning. Within-person analyses will separate session variation from between-person differences, accounting for dependent measurements and missing observations.</p></div><div><h4>Intended scientific contribution</h4><p>An account of affective dynamics distinguishing immediate relief, persistence of change and return to the earlier state.</p></div></div>

<h3 id="msc-mus-p08-title">Agency and psychological ownership</h3>
<p class="pub-question"><strong>Research question:</strong> Does access to different editing operations change perceived influence over the process and psychological ownership of the track?</p>
<div class="pub-body"><div><h4>Scope and methodology</h4><p>Planned random assignment to conditions differing in creative control, with comparable time and generator access. Logged operations and separate measures of agency, attributed contribution and ownership will address construct distinctness. Musical expertise will be considered.</p></div><div><h4>Intended scientific contribution</h4><p>A test of proposed control and agency mechanisms in human–AI interaction; psychological ownership does not imply legal authorship.</p></div></div>

<h3 id="msc-mus-p09-title">Music and narrative identity</h3>
<p class="pub-question"><strong>Research question:</strong> How might musical representation of experience relate to the interpretation of one’s life story?</p>
<div class="pub-body"><div><h4>Scope and methodology</h4><p>A planned comparison of autobiographical narratives and their musical representations will consider personal meaning, perspective change, narrative coherence and psychological distance. Analysis will distinguish subjective resonance from literal content fidelity. Sensitive materials will not be required.</p></div><div><h4>Intended scientific contribution</h4><p>An account of potential functions of musical externalisation in meaning-making and narrative identity, without assuming therapeutic efficacy.</p></div></div>
