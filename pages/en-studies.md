# Research programme

[PL](studies.md) · [EN](en-studies.md)

## Creators' experiences

Individual interviews will examine motives for creating music, the circumstances in which tracks are produced and the meanings participants attribute to music in relation to their personal experiences.

## Emotion regulation

A survey will investigate associations between the use of music generators, alexithymia, emotion regulation, rumination and well-being. The proposed AIM-ER model provides a theoretical basis for hypotheses about the mechanisms underlying these associations.

## Agency and authorship

Further studies will examine the role of personal contribution to music creation and perceived control over the finished work. We are also considering diary studies to examine emotional changes over time.

<p class="study-status">Study protocols are currently being developed.</p>

A critical synthesis of research on emotion regulation, expression and AI-assisted music creation. The framework will organise candidate mechanisms of the creative process and formulate testable hypotheses.</p></article>
<article class="pub" id="msc-mus-p02"><p class="pub-id">MSC-MUS-P02</p><h3>From personal experience to music</h3><p>Qualitative study. Interviews will examine how personal experiences inform lyrics, prompts and musical decisions, and the meanings creators attribute to the resulting tracks.</p></article>
<article class="pub" id="msc-mus-p03"><p class="pub-id">MSC-MUS-P03</p><h3>Motives and functions of music creation</h3><p>Survey study. Examination of motivations for using music generators and reported functions of creative activity, including expression, organising experiences and mood change. Motives will be distinguished from perceived effects.</p></article>
<article class="pub" id="msc-mus-p04"><p class="pub-id">MSC-MUS-P04</p><h3>Alexithymia and emotional expression</h3><p>Investigation of associations between difficulty identifying and describing emotions, emotional expression and clarity. The role of AI-assisted creation in these associations will be examined.</p></article>
<article class="pub" id="msc-mus-p05"><p class="pub-id">MSC-MUS-P05</p><h3>Measuring regulation functions</h3><p>Psychometric project. Development of the AI-MRFS scale informed by qualitative findings, followed by evaluation of its structure, reliability and validity. The instrument aims to distinguish regulation functions of AI-assisted music creation.</p></article>
<article class="pub" id="msc-mus-p06"><p class="pub-id">MSC-MUS-P06</p><h3>Rumination and avoidance</h3><p>Examination of associations between music creation, repetitive thinking about difficult experiences and emotional avoidance. The study considers diverse functions and consequences without presupposing harm.</p></article>
<article class="pub" id="msc-mus-p07"><p class="pub-id">MSC-MUS-P07</p><h3>Emotional changes in everyday life</h3><p>Diary study. Repeated affect measurements associated with creative sessions will enable analysis of within-person change and distinguish short-term changes from between-person differences.</p></article>
<article class="pub" id="msc-mus-p08"><p class="pub-id">MSC-MUS-P08</p><h3>Agency and perceived authorship</h3><p>Planned experiment. Comparison of conditions differing in creative control during AI-assisted music creation, focusing on perceived agency and psychological ownership of the resulting track.</p></article>
<article class="pub" id="msc-mus-p09"><p class="pub-id">MSC-MUS-P09</p><h3>Music and narrative identity</h3><p>Study of autobiographical representations in the context of music creation. Comparing narratives will help examine associations between musical externalisation and interpretation of personal life stories.</p></article>
