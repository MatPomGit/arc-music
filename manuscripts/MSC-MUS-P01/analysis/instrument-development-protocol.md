# Optional instrument development: separate research protocol

This is not a requirement for publishing the theoretical AIM-ER article. No scale is presented as validated and no data are reported.

## Design

Define modules for goals, available and executed operations, agency, ownership, resonance, clarity, affect and functioning. Treat categorical goals and behavioural logs as descriptive/formative indicators unless reflective measurement is justified. Recruit adult intended users with diverse musical expertise and AI experience. Conduct expert content review, cognitive interviews and iterative item revision in each language. Record comprehension problems and refusals; never reproduce licensed instruments without permission.

## Decision criteria before sampling

For candidate reflective modules, compare correlated-factor and plausible competing models using ordinal CFA with appropriate thresholds. Use independent development and confirmation samples. Flag items with weak loading (illustrative <.40), large residual associations, poorly occupied categories or differential functioning; retain or remove based on content and replicated evidence rather than a mechanical cutoff. Report omega with bootstrap confidence intervals and within-person reliability for repeated states. Test convergent, discriminant, incremental and longitudinal validity; do not infer distinctness from alpha. Establish configural, metric and scalar/threshold comparability where relevant; if unsupported, avoid pooled mean comparisons.

## Model-specific sample sizing

Once item counts and candidate models are fixed, simulate ordinal responses under plausible thresholds, loadings (.40–.80), factor correlations (.30–.90), group imbalance and 0–20% missingness. Vary sample sizes (e.g. 150, 250, 400, 600 per independent sample) and include at least 1,000 replications per design. Fit the actual planned estimator; report convergence, improper solutions, bias, 95% interval coverage and power for prespecified discriminant/invariance contrasts. Select the smallest feasible design meeting prespecified convergence (>=95%), absolute relative loading bias (<=10% where denominator meaningful), interval coverage (illustratively 92–98%), and >=80% power for a justified smallest meaningful departure from invariance. These thresholds are planning choices, not validated universal standards. Re-evaluate the criterion when pilot estimates are available. For repeated states independently vary number of participants, observations and ICC and fit the proposed multilevel measurement model. If the design is infeasible, narrow the module or use authorised existing measures. No sample size or validation success is claimed here.
