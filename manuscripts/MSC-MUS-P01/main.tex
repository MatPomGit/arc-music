\begin{abstract}
Generative artificial intelligence (AI) makes it possible to transform personal experiences into musical artefacts through prompting, generation, selection and revision, including for people without conventional composition skills. Existing research on music-related emotion regulation, expressive writing and human--AI co-creation provides plausible but incomplete explanations for the affective consequences of this practice. We propose AI-assisted Music for Emotion Regulation (AIM-ER), a process framework distinguishing the regulatory goal, creative operations, proximal psychological mechanisms and short- and longer-term outcomes. The framework specifies competing pathways involving affect labelling, psychological distance, reappraisal, agency, ownership, musical engagement and perseverative thinking. Its central proposition is that emotional consequences depend on the fit between the user's regulatory goal and the affordances of the creative process, rather than on AI use alone. Ten falsifiable propositions and complementary qualitative, cross-sectional, intensive longitudinal and experimental designs are presented. AIM-ER provides a research agenda for investigating when AI-assisted music creation supports emotional functioning, when it has little effect and when it may sustain maladaptive regulation. Its proposed contribution is a specific interaction between instructed regulatory goals and manipulable semantic affordances, assessed against matched exposure and alternative creative activities. The predictions distinguish perceived resonance, emotional clarity and functioning from immediate distress reduction. The framework also specifies limits of causal mediation and conditions under which a precise null or reversed interaction would weaken the account. It is intended as a theoretical basis for everyday, nonclinical creative practice.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} emotion regulation; generative AI; music psychology; creative agency; rumination; human--AI co-creation

\section{Introduction}
Music can elicit emotion, express a person's experience and serve deliberate regulatory purposes. These functions are related but not interchangeable: a listener may recognise sadness in a song without feeling sad, and may deliberately seek sad music without intending to increase distress. Research on musical mood regulation identifies distinct reasons for engaging with music \citep{saarikallio2007}; research on emotion induction identifies multiple routes from sound to felt emotion \citep{juslin2008}. Emotion regulation concerns changes in emotional experience or responding in relation to a goal, whereas emotion induction concerns the elicitation of an emotional response \citep{gross2015}. Consequently, the pleasantness or emotional intensity of a musical artefact is insufficient to establish its regulatory value.

Text-conditioned generation allows users to specify affective or aesthetic features without manually composing every musical element \citep{copet2023}. The creative process nevertheless includes decisions about lyrics, stylistic constraints, accepted outputs and revision. Early work on AI songwriting documents the importance of human selection and interaction with generative tools \citep{huang2020}. More recent research examines how users negotiate authorial identity \citep{zhao2026} and how automation can alter psychological ownership and state agency \citep{he2026}. A generated artefact can therefore be studied both as a musical stimulus and as the product of a sequence of user decisions. The distinction matters because a regulatory change could arise during articulation, selection or revision before the final listening episode.

Existing accounts of musical affect regulation distinguish goals, strategies and mechanisms \citep{baltazar2016,baltazar2019}, and the process model of emotion regulation provides a general account of the selection and implementation of regulatory strategies \citep{gross2015}. The unresolved question addressed here is narrower: whether opportunities to preserve or transform the meaning of an emotional experience have different regulatory consequences depending on the user's initial goal during generative music creation. The relevant comparison is not simply AI use versus non-use. For example, a person seeking to articulate an experience might value semantic fidelity, whereas a person seeking to reinterpret it might benefit from alternative perspectives. These are candidate effects rather than demonstrated advantages. The same operations could instead increase effort, reinforce perseverative thought or provide no additional benefit beyond writing and listening.

This distinction has practical and methodological implications. If immediate enjoyment, perceived resonance, emotional clarity and subsequent functioning are treated as interchangeable outcomes, a study cannot distinguish a satisfying creative experience from successful regulation. Immediate relief may coexist with later interference, and the absence of immediate relief need not imply unsuccessful articulation. A useful account must therefore specify the initial goal, the opportunity for action, the timing of the proposed psychological mechanism and the outcome against which success is evaluated. It must also permit null and adverse responses without treating them as exceptions to an assumed benefit.

The proposed AI-assisted Music for Emotion Regulation (AIM-ER) framework has three contributions. First, it organises established psychological constructs around an iterative creative session in which the user participates in producing the stimulus. Second, it specifies a central, directional prediction: fidelity-preserving operations should favour articulation and perspective-transforming operations should favour reinterpretation, relative to their respective mismatched conditions. This prediction is a proposed domain extension of existing regulatory accounts, not a claim that regulatory fit itself is novel or that generative AI is necessary. Third, it translates this prediction and ten complementary propositions into prospective comparisons that distinguish assignment effects, measured psychological mechanisms and observational associations. Matched exposure and equivalent non-AI creative operations provide tests of whether any incremental explanatory value is attributable to production, semantic control or a more general creative process.

The article is a theoretical and methodological contribution, not an empirical evaluation of efficacy. It concerns everyday, self-directed creative activity by adults; professional music therapy and clinical treatment require separate evidence and safeguards. No participant data were collected for this article. The formal and synthetic examples clarify predictions and design assumptions but do not validate the proposed mechanisms.

\section{Conceptual scope and evidence base}
AIM-ER denotes a person-directed process in which a generative music system contributes to a musical artefact and the person uses that activity in relation to an emotional goal. The goal can involve reducing unpleasant activation, sustaining positive affect, understanding an ambiguous emotion, expressing a difficult experience or tolerating it. Participants need not disclose autobiographical content; fictional material and instrumental music may serve analogous functions. Fully automated playback without a meaningful opportunity for human input is treated as a comparison condition, not as the prototypical creative process.

The present framework is a theory-building narrative synthesis. Its initial evidence base comprises foundational studies of regulation, musical emotion, expressive writing and motivation, complemented by research on generative music technology and co-creation. It distinguishes direct evidence about AI music-making from evidence transferred from related activities. This targeted selection does not establish exhaustive coverage or permit pooled effect estimates. The targeted evidence map dated 26 November 2026 records search terms, evidence classes, claims and verification status. Searches used general web discovery restricted where possible to publisher and PubMed sources. Inclusion prioritised conceptual predecessors, direct co-creation studies, transferable mechanisms and relevant measurement or design evidence. This was not a database-complete search; screening denominators and comprehensive citation chaining are unavailable. A prospective search and full-text claim audit across psychology, music and computing databases are required before submission. Abstract-only evidence is provisional.

\section{Theoretical foundations}
\subsection{Emotion regulation and musical engagement}
The process account of emotion regulation differentiates opportunities for changing situations, attention, appraisal and responses \citep{gross2015}. AI-assisted music creation can involve several of these operations within a single session. Choosing to create may redirect attention; revising lyrics may change appraisal; listening may modify affective activation. The operation performed and the regulatory strategy it serves should therefore be assessed separately. A prompt edit can either broaden perspective or intensify a negative interpretation.

\citet{juslin2008} distinguish mechanisms of musical emotion that include associations, memories and expectations. A generated song could recruit such processes through its musical features, autobiographical references or perceived relevance. However, musical pleasantness, emotional resonance and successful regulation are distinct outcomes. A piece can be aesthetically satisfying yet inconsistent with a person's regulatory goal. Evidence concerning neurochemical responses to music \citep{chanda2013} motivates investigation of reward and arousal, but does not establish corresponding neural changes during AI co-creation.

The extended process model distinguishes recognising a need to regulate, choosing a strategy and implementing it \citep{gross2015}. Musical affect regulation likewise distinguishes goals from strategies and the mechanisms through which an activity may affect experience \citep{baltazar2019}. AIM-ER applies these distinctions at the level of successive creative decisions. For example, changing a lyric is an observable operation, reinterpretation is a possible strategy, and a change in the meaning attributed to an event is a hypothesised psychological process. Identical operations may serve different goals, and different operations may serve the same goal. Logging a revision is therefore insufficient to classify its regulatory function. This mapping motivates separate assessment of intention, action, perceived meaning and outcome rather than inference from the artefact alone.

\subsection{Symbolic expression and psychological distance}
Expressive writing provides an adjacent paradigm for studying the transformation of personally meaningful experiences into language \citep{pennebaker1986}. Affect labelling research also suggests that verbal identification of emotions can alter responses to emotional material \citep{lieberman2007}. In AIM-ER, writing or editing a prompt may make an otherwise diffuse affective state more explicit. Turning a description into an external artefact may permit the creator to observe, revise or reconsider it. These processes are plausible routes to emotional clarity and reappraisal, but neither a generated lyric nor a change in its wording establishes that reappraisal occurred. Direct measures and appropriate controls are required.

Affect labelling and distancing should not be collapsed into a common mechanism. The affect labelling experiment of \citet{lieberman2007} examined neural responses to affective stimuli during a labelling task, not self-directed songwriting. Work on self-distancing \citep{kross2011} concerns changes in the perspective adopted when reflecting on personal experiences. These findings motivate, but do not validate, two potentially dissociable pathways in AIM-ER. A faithful rendering might improve the specificity of emotional expression without changing perspective. A transformed rendering might facilitate reinterpretation without increasing the precision of emotion labels. Both pathways may operate in a single session. A contrasting prediction is that semantic changes reduce resonance when faithful expression is the intended goal. Assess articulation, distance and interpretation separately, and distinguish perceived semantic fidelity from actual fidelity assessed by independent raters. These proposed dissociations concern regulatory operations, not a process unique to AI.

\subsection{Agency, ownership and creative control}
Self-determination theory provides a basis for distinguishing perceived autonomy and competence from mere task completion \citep{ryan2000}. AIM-ER distinguishes objective control (available and exercised operations), state agency (perceived influence over the process), psychological ownership (felt belonging of the artefact) and attributed authorship (perceived contribution). These constructs are not equivalent to legal authorship. A user may feel that an output represents their experience despite attributing much of its production to the system.

Qualitative findings on authorial positioning in AI-mediated music highlight the negotiation of creative identity \citep{zhao2026}. In a recent co-creation experiment, increased automation was associated with diminished ownership and state agency, with expertise-related differences \citep{he2026}. This is directly relevant to the control pathway, but it does not demonstrate an emotion-regulation effect. The exclusion of some assigned participants and the nonrandomised intermediate variables in that study limit causal interpretation of the proposed mediation chain. AIM-ER extends these accounts by predicting when perceived influence and personal resonance are related to goal-congruent affective changes. Excessive control demands may also introduce frustration; increasing the number of editable parameters need not monotonically improve regulation.

\subsection{Rumination and regulatory flexibility}
Rumination involves repetitive thought that can sustain negative affect and interfere with adaptive responses \citep{nolen2008}. Research on healthy and unhealthy patterns of music use indicates the importance of differentiating functions of engagement rather than treating exposure as uniformly beneficial \citep{hums2015}. A user who repeatedly generates variations of a distressing narrative may be engaging in constructive reflection, perseveration or both. The distinction depends on changes in perspective, persistence, flexibility and functioning, rather than the emotional tone of the lyrics alone. Music-related regulation and maladaptive patterns have been associated in behavioural and neuroimaging work \citep{carlson2015}; these findings do not establish that generating sad music causes rumination. Distinguish brooding from purposeful reflection. Three trajectories are especially informative: short-term relief followed by impaired functioning or avoidance; temporarily increased distress accompanied by improved understanding and later functioning; and sustained relief with preserved functioning. Track tolerability, unwanted effects and return to valued activities, not only valence. Regulatory flexibility further implies that a strategy's value depends on context and responsiveness to feedback \citep{bonanno2013}.

The flexibility account of \citet{bonanno2013} distinguishes sensitivity to situational demands, availability of alternative strategies and responsiveness to feedback. Applied to AIM-ER, these components imply that additional generation attempts are ambiguous behavioural indicators. Repeated attempts may represent adaptive exploration if the user revises a strategy in response to discrepancies, or perseveration if the user maintains an ineffective strategy despite accessible alternatives. The distinction motivates recording the decision-point goal, prior perceived attainment, available alternatives and the subsequent decision. A low number of attempts is likewise ambiguous because stopping may reflect goal attainment, frustration or an external constraint. The H9 prediction concerns transitions conditional on unsuccessful attempts and opportunity, not a general preference for fewer iterations. The H6 prediction concerns a measured state of repetitive negative thought, not the number of generated artefacts.

\subsection{Neuropsychological mechanisms: a multilevel account}
AIM-ER distinguishes four interacting neurocognitive functions: auditory-affective appraisal, reward learning, autobiographical retrieval and executive regulation. These are functional domains rather than a one-to-one mapping between psychological constructs and brain regions. The available neuroscience concerns primarily music perception and established emotion-regulation tasks, not the entire AI-assisted creative cycle.

\paragraph{Auditory-affective appraisal.} Musical structure provides temporally organised information from which listeners infer expressive features and form expectations. A distributed network involving auditory regions and structures implicated in affective processing supports musical emotion \citep{koelsch2014}. In an AI-assisted session, successive candidate outputs allow a person to compare perceived expression with the intended affective quality. A discrepancy may elicit revision, rejection or acceptance of an unexpected interpretation. Measure perceived emotion, experienced emotion and intended emotion separately; agreement between them is not required for adaptive regulation.

\paragraph{Reward and selection.} PET and fMRI evidence links anticipatory and peak pleasurable musical experiences to distinguishable striatal responses \citep{salimpoor2011}. Pharmacological modulation of dopaminergic function also changes musical reward experience \citep{ferreri2019}. These findings support a candidate account of repeated selection: anticipation of a satisfactory candidate motivates another attempt, whereas an accepted output provides consummatory pleasure. However, reward-related repetition does not guarantee goal attainment. A creator may continue generating because the next candidate could be more rewarding even after the initial emotional objective has been reached. Specific musical anhedonia further demonstrates that sensitivity to music-related reward varies independently of general reward responsiveness \citep{mas2014}. Measure musical reward sensitivity as a potential moderator, and distinguish anticipated enjoyment, achieved enjoyment, generation count and regulatory outcome. Neither behavioural engagement nor autonomic arousal is a direct measure of dopamine.

\paragraph{Autobiographical memory and narrative reconstruction.} \citet{janata2009} associated autobiographical salience during familiar music listening with activity in medial prefrontal regions. In AIM-ER, a newly generated piece can be deliberately constructed around a pre-existing memory, rather than retrieve memories solely through prior associations with familiar music. Repeated listening may subsequently establish a new association between the artefact and the experience. The two routes require distinct measures: initial autobiographical relevance, later familiarity, memory vividness and change in appraisal. Memories need not be disclosed to establish these variables. Personal resonance is not necessarily accurate autobiographical reconstruction, and intense recollection may either facilitate reflection or sustain distress.

\paragraph{Executive regulation and self-distancing.} Reappraisal recruits distributed cognitive control and semantic processes that alter emotional responding \citep{ochsner2012}. Self-distancing can support reflection without perseverative immersion \citep{kross2011}. A candidate AI-specific affordance is the rapid transformation of a personal narrative into alternative perspectives, voices or musical settings. A useful experiment compares first-person and observer-perspective renderings of equivalent non-sensitive content, with matched duration and independently rated semantic fidelity. Assess changes in psychological distance and interpretation before assessing delayed distress. An observer perspective is a manipulation, not proof of distancing; increased distance might also reflect disengagement or avoidance.

\paragraph{Evidence transfer.} The auditory and reward pathways have direct evidence for music perception, not generation; use matched playback and prospective reward ratings. Autobiographical retrieval findings concern familiar music; distinguish initial narrative relevance from later learned familiarity. Reappraisal and affect labelling have direct evidence in non-musical tasks; compare equivalent content across perspective and labelling manipulations. The proposed link from these operations to regulation in a generator workflow remains a hypothesis, and a neural correlate alone cannot distinguish the competing psychological explanations.

\paragraph{Psychophysiological measurement.} Electrodermal activity and cardiac measures may index changes in autonomic activation during generation, playback and recovery. Their interpretation depends on movement, respiration, sound level, attention and baseline activation. For example, compare matched silent decision periods and playback intervals, record respiration if heart rate variability is analysed, and prespecify artefact handling and minimum usable recording duration. EEG could examine auditory or attentional responses in a separate controlled study; neither scalp spectral power nor a frontal asymmetry score uniquely identifies emotional regulation, agency or reward. Neuroimaging and pharmacological methods are optional mechanistic extensions, not prerequisites for initial behavioural validation.

\subsection{Strength and limits of transferable evidence}
A meta-analysis of eight randomised studies involving 441 participants reported an overall standardised effect of $d=0.45$ for diverse musical activities on emotion regulation \citep{peters2024}. The interventions included listening, playing and creating, so the estimate cannot serve as the anticipated effect of AI-assisted generation relative to an active control. The trim-and-fill estimate was .40; with only eight studies, nonsignificant heterogeneity does not establish common effects across activities. Neuroimaging studies establish plausible biological substrates of specific musical or regulatory operations, whereas evidence from AI co-creation studies concerns the affordances of the interface. Neither line alone establishes the incremental regulatory benefit of generative systems. A recent critical review of algorithmically personalised and AI-generated music found very low certainty across assessed physiological, behavioural and clinical outcome domains, with few studies of actual generative models and no independent replication of specific findings \citep{jin2026}. Distinguish emotional conveyance from induction and regulation, and do not transfer results from passive algorithmic delivery to active generation. The strongest test of AIM-ER therefore combines controlled manipulation of a creative operation with temporally ordered psychological outcomes and an active comparator.

For theory evaluation, distinguish three levels of evidential proximity. Evidence about interaction with generative music systems supports specific claims about the availability and experience of creative operations; it does not by itself establish regulatory benefits. Evidence from music perception and non-musical emotion regulation supports the plausibility of component processes under the conditions studied, but its transfer to AI-assisted sessions remains an empirical question. The goal-specific interaction, the temporal coupling of mechanisms and outcomes, and the proposed adverse trajectories are AIM-ER predictions requiring their own tests. These levels describe directness to the target process, not a formal certainty rating. Methodological quality and replication must be evaluated separately. An observed component effect cannot establish the complete process model, and a favourable outcome alone cannot identify its mechanism.

\paragraph{Neuropsychological predictions and limits.} The articulation pathway is most directly relevant to H2 and H5, the appraisal and executive-regulation pathways to CP and H6, and reward and selection to H4 and H10. H3 concerns attribution and ownership rather than a specific neural substrate. H7--H9 concern social feedback, expertise and strategy switching; their neural implementation is not specified here. The decisive behavioural tests compare assigned operations and outcomes; neural or autonomic measures can provide convergent evidence but cannot substitute for them.

\section{The AIM-ER process model}
The unit of analysis is a creative session nested within a person. A session is defined by a predeclared start and assessment window around a personally relevant or experimentally assigned goal. Revisiting an artefact later is a distinct observation. Figure~\ref{fig:aim} represents the proposed temporal architecture.

\subsection{Stages and candidate mechanisms}
Initial conditions include baseline affect, goal, musical and AI experience, trait emotion regulation, available time and social context. The process comprises expression and prompting, generation, appraisal and selection, optional editing, and listening or sharing. The order can recur; the framework permits users to omit stages. A session begins at assignment or the logged intention to start and ends at the prespecified assessment window, even if a participant stops early or no usable output is produced. Record non-completion, its reason and output availability separately; assess outcomes regardless of completion. In a randomised study, non-completers remain in the assigned group. For instrumental music, expressive input may consist of a description or musical constraint rather than lyrics.

The proposed proximal mechanisms include emotional articulation, perceived distance from the experience, reappraisal, agency, ownership, perceived personal relevance and music-elicited affect. Frustration, repetitive negative thought and avoidance are competing pathways. Successful completion and liking are measured separately because they can explain apparent regulation benefits without the hypothesised mechanisms. Subsequent choices (repeat, revise, stop, switch activity or seek support) form a feedback loop.

\subsection{Goal--affordance fit}
The model distinguishes an affective target from the user's opportunity to implement a strategy. For instance, a person seeking emotional articulation may benefit from semantic fidelity and clear expressive options, whereas a person seeking reduced activation may benefit more from control over tempo, dynamics and listening duration. A person seeking a new interpretation may need editable semantic content rather than more stylistic variants. A mismatch can occur even when the musical quality is high.

A goal--affordance interaction is a stronger test of the framework than a main effect of generation. In a factorial experiment, independently assign an articulation versus reinterpretation instruction and a fidelity-preserving versus perspective-transforming interface. Assess goal-specific outcomes using separate validated or piloted measures, with a common goal-attainment outcome. The predicted interaction is a larger goal-attainment advantage for matched instruction--interface pairs than for mismatched pairs. Both factors must be successfully manipulated without changing exposure duration or access to the common underlying generator. The central prediction (CP), separate from H1--H10, requires both goal-specific matching effects to be positive, using an intersection-union test. The mean matching contrast summarises magnitude but does not establish the central prediction by itself. The primary outcome is a common goal-attainment score anchored at no attainment and complete attainment, with goal-specific clarity and reinterpretation as secondary outcomes. Test measurement comparability before contrasting the goals. An illustrative design threshold is 0.20 marginal standard deviations for the matching contrast; the final smallest effect of interest must be justified by participant anchors and measurement precision before data collection. A 90\% interval for the mean matching contrast wholly inside its prespecified equivalence band supports practical equivalence of the mean contrast, not equivalence of both simple effects; a 95\% interval wholly below zero supports a reversed effect. Wide intervals are inconclusive. The central claim concerns a conditional affordance effect and does not imply that AI is necessary for regulation.

\subsection{Temporal and formal specification}
Let $X_i$ denote stable individual characteristics, $C_{it}$ the context of session $t$ for person $i$, and $G_{it}$ the pre-session regulatory goal. Let $j$ index successive decision steps and $G_{it0}=G_{it}$ be the initial goal. With affordance assignment $Z$, operations $A_j$, candidate output $O_j$ (including a null output), appraisal $P_j$ and mechanism state $M_j$, suppress the person and session indices for clarity:
\begin{align}
 A_j &= f_A(Z,G_{j-1},P_{j-1},X,C,\epsilon_j^A),\\
 O_j &= f_O(A_j,Z,O_{j-1},\epsilon_j^O),\\
 (P_j,M_j)&= f_M(O_j,A_j,G_{j-1},M_{j-1},X,C,\epsilon_j^M),\\
 G_j &= f_G(G_{j-1},P_j,M_j,C,\epsilon_j^G),\\
 Y_{itk}&=f_Y(A_{1:J},O_{1:J},M_{1:J},G_{it0},X_i,C_{it},Y_{it0},\eta_{itk}).
\end{align}
Here $J$ is the observed number of steps (possibly zero), and the last equation applies only for $k\in\{1,2\}$: the end-of-session and follow-up assessments. Baseline is $k=0$. Stop, switch and continue are observable decisions, not missing steps; the initial goal defines the primary contrast, while later goal changes are process outcomes. The session history is not a set of adjustment covariates for an intention-to-treat analysis. The equations express dependencies, not identification assumptions or fixed functional forms. In naturalistic data, baseline distress and unmeasured contextual factors may influence both the choice of activity and its outcome. A statistical path between a measured mechanism and an outcome does not by itself establish mediation.

For a down-regulation goal, define immediate relief $R_{it}=D_{it0}-D_{it1}$ and delayed relief $L_{it}=D_{it0}-D_{it2}$, where $D$ is a prespecified distress measure with higher values indicating more distress. For other goals, report valence, arousal, clarity and goal attainment separately rather than applying the relief score indiscriminately. Immediate relief and delayed relief can have opposite signs. The framework predicts that their joint distribution is more informative than either outcome alone.

\section{Falsifiable propositions}
The central research question is whether the effect of an assigned semantic affordance on immediate attainment of an assigned emotional goal differs in the predicted direction for articulation and reinterpretation. The central prediction (CP) requires positive matching effects within both goal conditions, as defined in the identification section. Secondary questions concern the incremental contribution of musical rendering, creative control and social feedback; individual differences in emotional clarity, perseveration and regulatory flexibility; and the temporal stability of enjoyment. The ten propositions below preserve these distinct questions. Each is a candidate for a separate, preregistered test, not a simultaneous confirmatory battery.

The propositions fall into three complementary groups: incremental effects of creative operations (H1, H2, H4, H7), individual and contextual differences (H3, H5, H8, H9), and temporal trajectories (H6, H10). None is a necessary component of the CP conjunction, and their confirmation cannot substitute for its direct factorial test.

\begin{description}
\item[H1: Affect.] In adults pursuing relief from everyday negative affect, assignment to self-directed AI music creation rather than a duration-matched neutral activity predicts lower immediate distress, adjusted for baseline distress. The primary contrast is the assigned-group difference, with negative values favouring creation.
\item[H2: Musical contribution.] Following the same expressive writing procedure, assignment to hearing a generated musical rendering rather than reading one's own text predicts higher immediate personal emotional resonance. Distress reduction is a separate outcome. The contrast concerns the rendering package, not the unique effect of AI.
\item[H3: Authorship.] At comparable objective control, greater perceived authorial contribution is positively associated with subsequently measured psychological ownership. Distinct measures and prespecified adjustment variables are required; the association does not identify a causal effect of attribution.
\item[H4: Control.] Assignment to an interface offering meaningful editing rather than selection only predicts higher immediate state agency before sharing, with the same generator and time budget. The primary contrast concerns the offered opportunity, not the amount of editing actually performed.
\item[H5: Alexithymia.] Higher baseline trait difficulty identifying emotions predicts lower post-session emotional clarity conditional on baseline clarity. General alexithymia, unaided articulation and moderation by labelling prompts are distinct constructs or secondary questions; they cannot substitute for the specified association.
\item[H6: Rumination.] Within a person, higher state rumination after the immediate assessment predicts lower delayed relief at the prespecified follow-up, conditional on baseline distress and immediate relief. The expected within-person coefficient is negative; between-person differences and causal mediation are separate questions.
\item[H7: Social response.] Assignment to supportive rather than neutral standardised feedback about a non-sensitive artefact predicts both higher immediate perceived social validation and higher subsequent goal attainment. The two outcomes must be distinguished; improvement in validation alone does not establish improved goal attainment.
\item[H8: Expertise.] The assigned-group contrast in state agency for reduced versus greater creative control becomes more negative with increasing baseline musical expertise. The primary target is the continuous expertise-by-assignment interaction, not a contrast between arbitrarily dichotomised expertise groups.
\item[H9: Flexibility.] Greater baseline regulatory flexibility predicts a higher conditional probability of switching or stopping rather than repeating after a prespecified unsuccessful decision point at which alternatives are available. Estimate the combined transition probability and describe switching and stopping separately; the association does not establish their adaptiveness.
\item[H10: Novelty.] In a repeated-task comparison of assigned fixed-version AI and comparator tools, the between-arm difference in mean first-to-last changes in immediate enjoyment is predicted to be negative (AI minus comparator). A decrease is compatible with a novelty account but does not identify novelty as its cause. Repeated novelty ratings and goal attainment are separate outcomes.
\end{description}

Directional predictions and practically meaningful effects are different inferential targets. Before confirmatory recruitment, specify the outcome orientation, population, assessment window, analysis model and smallest effect of interest for each primary estimand. For a single contrast, an interval wholly in the predicted direction supports that directional prediction under the prespecified error rate; an interval wholly in the opposite direction challenges it. A formal equivalence test within prospectively justified bounds can establish evidence of a practically negligible effect, whereas an imprecise interval spanning meaningful effects in both directions is inconclusive. The primary H6 error rate remains the calibrated candidate described below. For CP, both component tests must satisfy the intersection-union rule; the mean contrast alone cannot establish the prediction. A precisely negligible or reversed component challenges the practically meaningful crossover even when the average contrast is positive. Component-level equivalence or reversal claims require their own prespecified decision rules and error control. For H7, if both outcomes are to be asserted jointly as confirmatory, a conjunction rule and joint power analysis should be specified; otherwise report the individual results without treating partial support as confirmation of the entire proposition. These decision rules require prospective approval and measurement adequacy. Failure of an instrument, an affordance manipulation or outcome ascertainment limits interpretation of a test rather than directly refuting the underlying mechanism.

\section{Operationalisation and validation programme}
The companion programme includes qualitative discovery, survey and psychometric development, observational diaries and assigned-task comparisons. These projects do not currently constitute exact dedicated tests of the complete central prediction or H1 to H10. P04 uses general alexithymia rather than a difficulty-identifying-emotions score; P06 measures next-day functional interference rather than delayed distress relief; P08 manipulates decision authority and execution, with expertise moderation exploratory. P07 examines natural creation windows and valence, not randomized relief or novelty. P09 compares representational packages using event-specific understanding, not the incremental resonance effect of a common written text. P02, P03 and P05 provide contextual and measurement development evidence rather than confirmation. A proposition-to-protocol matrix specifies the required constructs, populations, timings, estimands and gaps for all eleven predictions. No companion protocol or synthetic result constitutes empirical validation of AIM-ER.

\subsection{Qualitative discovery and cross-sectional study}
Purposeful interviews with adult users should include those reporting benefits, ambivalence, no effects and adverse experiences. Interview prompts should focus on a concrete recent episode, initial goal, operations, perceived contribution, immediate and subsequent consequences, and alternatives considered. Reflexive thematic analysis can identify missing mechanisms and divergent experiences. The interview sample is not a prevalence sample and does not determine the direction of quantitative effects.

A cross-sectional survey may examine associations among use motives, difficulties identifying emotions, ownership, rumination and well-being. Recruitment through creator communities limits external validity and may overrepresent enthusiastic or highly engaged users. Exposure, self-reported outcomes and prior mental health cannot be assumed temporally ordered. Any mediation analysis using these data is descriptive. Scale adaptations require language-specific psychometric evidence, permissions and a separate evaluation of reliability and dimensionality. The development of a dedicated regulatory-function instrument is a distinct psychometric project.

\subsection{Intensive longitudinal study}
A prospective diary design can separate between-person differences from within-person fluctuations. Collect baseline ratings immediately before self-initiated sessions, process measures at the end and follow-up ratings at a fixed interval (for example 24 hours), together with comparable non-creation observations where feasible. Record other regulatory activities, elapsed time, session duration, generator and interface version, and whether the participant revisits the output. Avoid capturing raw prompts or audio by default.

For H6, let $Q_{it}$ denote state rumination assessed after immediate relief and before the delayed outcome, and $\bar Q_i$ the participant mean in a retrospective explanatory model. A candidate mixed model for delayed relief is
\begin{align}
L_{it}={}&\alpha+u_i+\beta_W(Q_{it}-\bar Q_i)+\beta_B\bar Q_i\nonumber\\
&+\gamma R_{it}+\delta D_{it0}+\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\top}C_{it}+e_{it}.
\end{align}
The within-person coefficient $\beta_W$ is predicted to be negative. The primary marginal regression uses participant-cluster CR2 uncertainty and contrast-specific Satterthwaite degrees of freedom (two-sided $\alpha=.04$); the mixed model is a sensitivity analysis. The candidate .04 threshold was selected in synthetic design development to reduce null rejection under the examined MAR scenario (.037 at this threshold); it is not a universal correction for clustering or missingness. Under the examined MNAR scenario the rejection rate remained .067. Independently recalibrate the prespecified analysis and decision rule over plausible designs before confirmatory use; report the nominal threshold and uncertainty explicitly. Interpret the coefficient as an adjusted association, not a causal effect. Where follow-up timing varies, specify a window, record actual delay and test robustness to deviations. Missing follow-up may depend on distress or disengagement; compare observed and missing cases on available baseline variables and conduct sensitivity analyses for departures from missing at random. Estimate uncertainty with participant-level dependence respected. Decompose session-varying predictors into within- and between-person components; add $u_{Qi}(Q_{it}-\bar Q_i)$ with correlated random intercept and slope, prior-session distress and rumination, and residual correlation $\phi^{|t-s|}$ for equally spaced sessions (continuous-time decay for irregular intervals). A sensitivity model predicting $D_{it2}$ with $D_{it0}$ and $D_{it1}$ has the opposite sign on the rumination coefficient under the equivalent complete-data linear parameterisation. Examine lagged rumination and follow-up timing; for prospective prediction use only past observations to construct person means. Compare MAR likelihood, estimated observation weights and pattern-mixture shifts with participant-bootstrap intervals; under MNAR, treat conclusions sensitive to plausible shifts as inconclusive. Shared baseline measurement error and conditioning on post-session relief limit interpretation; the coefficient is not a causal effect.

\subsection{Candidate protocol for the central prediction}
The primary validation study is a parallel-group experiment with four equally allocated conditions. The target population comprises adults able to provide informed consent, understand the study language and complete a brief digital music task. Recruitment should include people with different levels of musical and generative AI experience, rather than only established creator-community members. The initial study should use a standardised fictional or mild, non-sensitive emotional scenario. It does not recruit on the basis of a clinical diagnosis or require personal disclosure. Record eligibility, refusal and recruitment channel before randomisation. The unit of randomisation and primary analysis is the participant, with one assigned session per participant. Determine the recruitment target only after feasibility work, measurement evaluation and joint-power recalibration for CP; the synthetic candidate sizes below are not approved targets.

After consent and baseline affect, experience and expectations are recorded, use a computer-generated allocation sequence concealed until eligibility and baseline assessment are complete. Independently assign goal (articulation or reinterpretation) and semantic affordance (fidelity preservation or perspective transformation). Provide a brief common orientation to the interface. In the articulation condition, ask participants to express the emotional meaning of the scenario accurately. In the reinterpretation condition, ask them to explore a different plausible interpretation of the same scenario. Both interfaces should offer a comparable number of decisions, with one designed to preserve the original meaning and the other to permit a change of perspective. Neither interface should be described as preferable. Use the same input scenario pool, generator version, candidate-generation budget, response format and session time across conditions. A feasibility study must establish that both interfaces permit meaningful user input and implement their intended semantic operations. Participants cannot be blinded to the task instructions; conceal the predicted matching pattern and blind independent outcome coders to assignment where feasible.

The apparatus is a computer-based research interface with controlled access to an approved generation service and standardised audio playback. Record the model and interface version, generation latency, attempted and successful operations, selection or rejection, technical failures and early termination with pseudonymous identifiers. Use identical playback settings within each session and a safe participant-adjustable volume, recording adjustments where feasible. Freeze the software and task materials before confirmatory recruitment. If the generator changes during a study, document the affected sessions and evaluate the impact according to a prespecified version-change rule rather than silently combining incompatible implementations.

The assessment sequence is baseline, assignment and instructions, pre-task expectancy, creative operations, immediate goal attainment, secondary process and affect ratings, and a delayed follow-up. Obtain the primary attainment rating before detailed questions about potential mechanisms to reduce demand priming. Obtain it at the scheduled end of the session even if no usable artefact was produced, because the endpoint concerns the assigned goal rather than product quality. An absent artefact is a process outcome, not a missing participant; a missing rating is not automatically scored zero. Artefact-specific ratings may be undefined when no artefact exists and require a separately specified estimand or descriptive analysis. Delayed functioning and affect are secondary for CP. The primary analysis retains assigned groups and does not exclude participants on the basis of manipulation-check responses, achieved editing, artefact quality or session completion.

Before confirmatory use, run cognitive interviews and feasibility sessions to evaluate instruction comprehension, semantic separation, rating interpretation, completion and technical stability. Have independent raters assess semantic preservation and perspective transformation using a prospective rubric on approved fictional materials, with agreement and uncertainty reported. Match or constrain acoustic features and candidate budgets during design rather than adjusting for achieved quality after randomisation. Prespecify acceptable manipulation fidelity, assessment completion, software reliability and measurement comparability in the feasibility protocol. Failure to meet a criterion calls for revision and another feasibility assessment, not selective exclusion of participants from a confirmatory analysis. The primary factorial test addresses matching within the generative setting; equivalent non-AI production and yoked playback are separate comparisons, not conditions implicitly included in the four-cell design.

\subsection{Experimental decomposition}
A first experiment can isolate the effect of adding a generated musical rendering to a common expressive task. Randomise adults after a standardised mild, non-sensitive writing task to (A) reading their own text, (B) listening to a musical rendering of that text generated using a standardised procedure, or (C) listening to a duration-matched, non-personal musical excerpt. Use the same time budget, comparable playback levels and matched instructions. Condition B versus A estimates the incremental effect of the rendering package, while B versus C addresses personalisation combined with text-content differences; neither isolates AI from musical composition generally. An additional human-composed rendering arm is necessary to isolate differences associated with the production method, and requires appropriate matching.

For H4 a separate randomised comparison assigns an editable versus selection-only interface while holding model, input material, exposure time and available candidate outputs as constant as feasible. State agency is assessed before any social sharing. Report the intention-to-treat contrast and manipulation checks separately; do not condition the primary estimate on achieved editing behaviour. Measure expectation, quality and perceived effort, but avoid adjusting the total effect for post-randomisation variables.

For a distress-reduction experiment addressing H1, predefine the population and comparator, and randomise at the point of eligibility. The primary estimand is the mean difference in immediate distress under assignment to AI creation versus neutral activity, with baseline distress adjustment for precision. A two-arm analysis can use
\begin{equation}
D_{i1}=\alpha+\tau Z_i+\gamma D_{i0}+\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\top}X_i+e_i,
\end{equation}
where $Z_i=1$ denotes AI creation, and $\tau<0$ favours creation. Prespecify covariates, a smallest effect of interest, a two-sided confidence interval, attrition handling and a power calculation tied to the primary endpoint. For H2 the primary endpoint is resonance; H1 and H2 thus need distinct power calculations or a justified multiple-testing plan. Randomise H1 after baseline; use an active non-AI comparator in a subsequent trial. For H2 add a spoken rendering when feasible to separate modality and performance from composition, use a common resonance scale, and obtain blinded fidelity and quality ratings. Record expectations before assignment and after the instruction separately. Use a prespecified multiplicity correction for additional confirmatory contrasts, with exploratory mechanism analyses labelled accordingly. Tests of mediation need temporal separation and explicit assumptions about mediator--outcome confounding; where possible manipulate the proposed mechanism directly.

\subsection{Additional proposition-specific contrasts}
For H3 measure attributed contribution before ownership, with non-overlapping items; compare the adjusted association with the reverse temporal order, controlling prior experience and effort without causal attribution. H5 uses a trait difficulty-identifying-emotions measure and distinct state clarity and unaided articulation outcomes; account for baseline clarity, negative affect and depressive symptoms. H7 assigns opt-in participants to standardised supportive or neutral responses of comparable length and source after a fictional task, assesses validation immediately and goal attainment at a fixed follow-up, and does not imply that actual peers wrote the messages. H8 uses continuous musical expertise and its interaction with randomised interface assignment; assess digital audio workstation familiarity, AI experience and musical identity at baseline. Report conditional contrasts across the observed range without dichotomising expertise. H9 defines an unsuccessful session by a prespecified attainment threshold at the decision point, records available alternatives and separates repeating, switching and stopping (including time-limited and intentional stopping). Analyse competing transitions among eligible decisions, check threshold sensitivity and interpret the conditional association as predictive rather than causal. H10 randomises fixed-version tools with comparable tasks. Offer the final standardised session independently of intermediate attendance. The primary contrast is the between-arm difference of mean first-to-last enjoyment changes. Regress change on first-session enjoyment separately by arm and standardise to each arm's own first-rating distribution, with influence-function uncertainty. This post-assignment rating is not a pretreatment covariate. Under MAR and correct outcome regression, retain the assigned population; model missing first ratings separately and assess arm-specific shifts for missing endpoints. Repeated-session trends, novelty and regulation are secondary outcomes; novelty mediation is not identified.

\section{Competing explanations and boundary conditions}
The framework would be weakened if well-controlled comparisons consistently found that matched listening, writing or general creative engagement explains the observed emotional changes with no meaningful contribution of control, personal relevance or their proposed interactions. This need not invalidate the usefulness of music itself. Null results with broad intervals are inconclusive; equivalence claims require a prespecified interval of practically negligible effects.

Alternative explanations include expectancy, demand characteristics, regression to the mean following self-selection at high distress, novelty, generic distraction, musical preference, time away from stressors and social recognition. A useful test compares AIM-ER predictions with a simpler baseline model using these factors and baseline affect. Out-of-sample predictive performance may support incremental descriptive utility, but not causal identification.

The effects may depend on platform affordances, cultural expectations, language, sensory preferences, previous musical expertise and reasons for use. A model version change can alter both output quality and available control; a study should record these changes and avoid silently pooling incompatible sessions. Clinical populations and traumatic experiences require separate safety protocols and cannot be inferred from convenience samples.

\section{Ethics and open research}
Participation should be voluntary and based on informed consent, with the option to omit questions, stop a task or decline sharing any artefact. The initial validation programme should use mild material rather than require disclosures of trauma. A protocol should specify procedures for distress, withdrawal, and access to appropriate support. No unapproved identifiable or sensitive personal text should be submitted to external generation services. The generator's data retention, training use, licensing and age restrictions require assessment before study deployment. Start with adults and fictional or participant-approved minimal inputs. The data flow is consent and local task input, approved generation service only when necessary, pseudonymous operation logs and outcomes, restricted institutional storage, and disclosure-reviewed aggregate export. Specify retention and deletion windows, withdrawal limits for anonymised aggregates, data-controller and processor responsibilities, breach escalation and opt-out from sharing. Review input and output rights before dissemination. Obtain institutional ethics approval before recruitment; this theoretical proposal reports no approval or clinical efficacy. Research data and consent documentation should be separated and held in access-controlled institutional storage; share only approved materials, analysis code and disclosure-reviewed summaries. Preregister confirmatory hypotheses and analysis decisions before recruitment. Preserve distinctions between pilot, exploratory and confirmatory work.

Before any recruitment, the research team must confirm the institutional ethics decision, approved consent and withdrawal procedures, instrument and stimulus rights, data-controller and processor arrangements, permitted generator inputs and retention settings, and a locked protocol and analysis plan. Record which approvals are required, granted or outstanding; do not infer approval from the existence of this manuscript or simulation code. The task must be interruptible without penalty, including during generation or playback. Where feasible, offer an optional post-interruption outcome assessment separately from the decision to stop the task; participants retain the right to decline. The consent procedure must distinguish stopping the activity from withdrawing data and explain the approved limits of withdrawal. Use a prespecified response to distress, with no requirement to describe the underlying experience. Store the pseudonymisation key and consent records separately from analysis data in approved institutional storage; do not publish raw prompts, audio or individual-level records in the repository. Any public artefact sharing requires a separate rights and disclosure review.

\section{Research transparency}
No participant data were collected for this theoretical article. The equations and simulation code are intended to clarify design assumptions and are accompanied by reproducible computational examples. The availability of participant data, ethical approvals and preregistration will be reported for subsequent empirical studies.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Theoretical contribution and discriminating evidence}
The principal theoretical contribution is a restricted prediction about semantic affordances during goal-directed creative production. Existing accounts of emotion regulation and regulatory fit already explain why goals and strategies matter; AIM-ER specifies an operational contrast between preserving and transforming meaning in a generator workflow. A positive main effect of generation, enjoyment or perceived agency would not establish this prediction. Both directional simple effects must be supported. A matching advantage under equivalent non-AI editing would support a general creative-regulation account rather than technological specificity. A matching advantage confined to active generation rather than yoked playback would support the production pathway, subject to differences in agency and expectancy between the tasks. These comparisons address distinct claims and should not be collapsed into a single efficacy conclusion.

\subsection{Interpretation of heterogeneous and adverse outcomes}
Greater control may support agency while increasing cognitive effort or frustration. Emotional resonance may accompany either articulation or repetitive negative thinking. Immediate relief, improved understanding and delayed functioning therefore need separate assessments. A trajectory of immediate relief followed by interference in valued activities would not establish adaptive regulation. Likewise, a temporary increase in distress accompanied by improved understanding is not sufficient evidence of long-term benefit. The proposed follow-up and comparison activities are needed to distinguish these possibilities. H7 and H10 describe potentially general social and novelty processes, not mechanisms unique to generative music; they inform boundary conditions rather than the core matching claim.

\subsection{Limitations and next validation steps}
The narrative evidence base does not provide an exhaustive or quantitative synthesis, and direct co-creation evidence remains limited. The current model is a set of hypotheses, not a validated instrument or clinical intervention. The common attainment rating requires response-process evidence across instructions. Gaussian and bounded-outcome calibrations are complementary, not interchangeable: the latter shows markedly larger conditional sample requirements for .30 raw-point effects and potentially extreme false-positive rates under differential MNAR. Neither nominal power nor the simulated error rates establish validity outside the examined data-generating models. The first empirical steps are cognitive interviews, a feasible and ethically approved manipulation pilot, and a measurement study. Only after these steps should the team justify practically relevant component effects, recalibrate the joint decision using pilot-informed bounded-outcome and missingness models, assess tipping points and identification bounds, and determine feasible recruitment requirements. The proposed studies address complementary questions, not a single confirmatory battery.

\section{Conclusion}
AIM-ER identifies the process of translating an emotional intention into a personally meaningful musical artefact as a candidate regulatory mechanism. Its distinctive prediction concerns the interaction of user goals, creative affordances and psychological response, not the mere availability of generated music. It accommodates both beneficial and adverse trajectories and specifies how to distinguish musical exposure, expression, perceived control and authorship. The framework offers a basis for staged empirical validation across complementary methods. Its value will depend on whether these mechanisms explain and predict outcomes beyond simpler alternatives, with meaningful effects that replicate across users, tasks and technological contexts.
