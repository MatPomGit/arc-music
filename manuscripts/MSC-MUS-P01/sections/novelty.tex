\subsection{Relation to competing accounts}
The distinction among goals, strategies, tactics and mechanisms predates AIM-ER \citep{baltazar2016,baltazar2019}. The framework therefore does not claim a new general principle of regulatory fit. Table~\ref{tab:competing} contrasts explanatory scope and empirical commitments. The domain extension is the production of a stimulus through iterative human--generator decisions; a proposed mechanistic extension is the interaction of specific semantic operations with instructed goals, beyond preference, pleasure or global agency. This extension is not exclusive to AI and could fail even if existing regulation theories remain useful.

Fidelity preservation should favour articulation, whereas perspective transformation should favour reinterpretation. Attenuation under yoked playback would support a production pathway; replication with equivalent non-AI editing would indicate a domain-general mechanism. A precise absent or reversed matching advantage would weaken this extension, without refuting existing accounts.

A particularly relevant experimental precedent is \citet{baltazarstress2019}. In a counterbalanced within-person factorial study with 34 analysed participants, individually selected music and regulation strategies judged helpful or unhelpful for calming down were crossed after stress induction. Both factors influenced reported tension; the combination judged helpful on both dimensions was more favourable than the combination judged unhelpful on both, but did not consistently outperform the mixed conditions. This establishes that factorial decomposition of musical resources and regulatory strategies predates AIM-ER. It does not test the proposed crossover between distinct assigned goals and semantic editing opportunities: the earlier study used one calming goal and existing recordings rather than participant-directed generation. AIM-ER therefore extends the experimental question to production operations and competing goals, not to the general existence of music-strategy fit. The earlier estimates do not determine the expected magnitude of either simple effect in the central prediction.

\subsection{Recent direct evidence and overlap}
\citet{he2026} manipulated automation in a music co-creation task and examined task load, ownership and state agency across expertise groups. This precedes the control and expertise propositions. \citet{zhang2026} randomly assigned young adults with high alexithymic traits to two songwriting workflows. The accepted abstract reports advantages of user-directed creation for ownership, perceived expressive authenticity and subsequent unaided articulation, but no detectable between-workflow difference in negative-affect change. The latter is not evidence of equivalence. Assignment varied revision, engagement and time as well as control; the intermediate measures were not randomised, and neither a non-AI nor a non-songwriting control was included. The full article was not yet available at the evidence update on 26 November 2026.

\subsection{Residual research question and scope of novelty}
The reviewed studies support different levels of inference. The musical regulation literature supplies the conceptual and experimental precedent for matching resources to regulatory needs. Interface studies address the availability and use of creative operations. In particular, the PAGURI reanalysis \citep{zhangpan2026} uses the corpus from \citet{ronchini2025}, not an independent replication; its role categories and lexical indicators do not establish validated expertise, psychological control or emotion regulation. The accepted songwriting study \citep{zhang2026} reports an assigned-workflow contrast in a selected high-alexithymia population. Its absence of a detectable differential change in negative affect is compatible with several explanations and does not establish practical equivalence. Taken together, these accounts motivate tests of expressive control but do not resolve whether distinct semantic operations have opposite relative advantages across articulation and reinterpretation goals.

The residual question is defined operationally rather than as a claim of unprecedented technology or theory: does independently assigning the regulatory goal and the semantic operation yield both predicted matching advantages on a comparable attainment measure? The target contrast requires common generator access, a comparable decision budget and ascertainment of outcomes regardless of successful generation. An active non-AI comparison would address generality across production methods, and a yoked listening comparison would address the contribution of active production. A positive result in the primary factorial study alone would not demonstrate AI specificity or identify a psychological mediator. This synthesis is targeted rather than exhaustive; the identified gap is limited to the evidence examined and must be reassessed against subsequent publications before submission.

\subsection{Limits of transfer from receptive and active music research}
The systematic review of experimental listening studies by \citet{adiasto2022} indicates uncertainty about short-term stress recovery: after exclusion of an outlier, the pooled standardized effect was $g=0.18$ (95\% CI $[-0.18,0.54]$). The synthesis of musical engagement by \citet{peters2024} addresses a broader class of activities and regulation measures, reporting an overall effect of $d=0.45$ across eight studies ($p<.01$). The estimates differ in intervention scope, outcomes and effect-size definitions; their numerical difference is not a test of active creation versus listening. These reviews do not estimate the same intervention effect and neither identifies the contribution of generative semantic operations. Their findings motivate separate assessment of stress recovery, goal attainment and subsequent functioning, with a receptive comparison that matches musical exposure. In particular, an advantage of active creation over listening would not by itself identify an effect specific to AI; an equivalent non-AI production comparison remains necessary.
