\subsection{Preliminary computational evidence (synthetic only)}
A development run (GitHub Actions 45894186937, source commit \texttt{b3590c4}, verified archive 15152208946) examined simplified generated datasets. For nine-item EFA with the generating factor count supplied, moderate loadings (.65) and correlation (.35) yielded 12/12 proper solutions in each of two scenarios; weak loadings (.40), high correlation (.80) and skew yielded only 3/12. This is not a test of factor-count recovery.

For eight-item robust DWLS at $N=500$ with moderate loadings and correlations, the first loading interval covered 27/30 generating values (Wilson 95\% interval .744--.965), while the first factor correlation interval covered 23/30. The latter is below the intended coverage range. In group-comparability simulations, the loading-restriction test detected a loading violation in only 11/30 datasets. These results identify unresolved inferential and power limitations; they do not justify recruitment targets. With 35 independent item-missingness events at rate .10, the expected complete-record fraction is only $.9^{35}$, approximately .025. Complete-record analysis cannot be the sole strategy for such scenarios. A reproducible sharded calibration grid has been implemented, but no candidate sample size has been approved. Larger simulations, broader reference comparisons and an adequate missing-data strategy remain prerequisites for confirmatory freeze.

\paragraph{Diagnosing the weak, highly correlated model.}
The 35-item model is locally identified at the exact generating moments
(56 free association parameters and full Jacobian rank). At a loading
of .40 the within-domain latent response correlation is .16, versus .128
between domains when the factor correlation is .80. An analytic Jacobian
and a third, correlated start reproduced the historical 0/8 proper
solutions. Seven fits reached a Cholesky bound with the smallest factor
eigenvalue between .00023 and .00394, while one did not converge.
The estimated uniquenesses in the seven converged fits exceeded .70.
These diagnostics point to weak empirical separation of factors in the
simulated samples.

\paragraph{Reference comparison.}
For identical complete synthetic records we compared the development
estimator with \texttt{lavaan} WLSMV \parencite{rosseel2012}, fixing factor
variances to one, using the delta parameterization and aligning factor
signs to the first item in each domain. For one 35-item sample with
moderate loadings ($N=500$), absolute differences were at most .006 for
loadings and factor correlations and .003 for their standard errors.
An earlier development comparison reported agreement in a selected weak case and a minimum eigenvalue of $-.00132$ for the historical draw. The later archived comparison (CI 45990267517, artifact 15193905033, \texttt{lavaan} 0.6-21, Python 3.12.16) differs: in the moderate 35-item case maximum differences were .00495 for loadings, .00461 for factor correlations and .00191 for standard errors. At weak seed 69, the reference passed its post-check (minimum eigenvalue .01522) while the development estimator rejected the fit. For the first historical weak draw, both failed admissibility; the reference minimum eigenvalue was $-.05252$ despite convergence and a scaled $p$-value of .59483. The versioned outcomes must not be conflated. The weak-model discrepancy remains unresolved; it does not support model readiness. The new comparison reports all cases and separates core parameter agreement, numerical execution and admissibility disagreement. The moment-matched residual statistic and WLSMV statistic use
different corrections. Their $p$-values are reported separately.

At $N=800$ with weak loadings and highly correlated domains, 4/12 proper fits correspond to an estimated Monte Carlo standard error of .136 and Wilson 95\% interval of .138--.609. Even 12/12 successes imply a lower Wilson limit of .758. A seemingly acceptable conditional coverage rate cannot compensate for a high failure probability. The contrast at $N=800$ suggests that joint weak measurement and high domain overlap, rather than sample size alone, drive the observed instability. This motivates evaluation of domain content and a separately frozen model if overlapping domains require revision. The screen does not justify choosing $N=800$, merging domains, or claiming that any proposed structure has empirical validity. The full candidate-size grid and independent EFA/CFA remain necessary.
