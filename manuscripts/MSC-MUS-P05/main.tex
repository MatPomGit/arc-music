\begin{abstract}
AI-assisted music creation allows users to translate intentions into musical artefacts through prompting, selection and revision. We propose the AI-Music Regulation Functions Scale (AI-MRFS), an episode-anchored self-report measure of perceived regulatory functions, rather than therapeutic efficacy or stable personality. A provisional seven-domain item pool will be refined using qualitative evidence, expert review and cognitive interviews with Polish-speaking adult creators. Separate exploratory and confirmatory samples will test ordinal latent structure, score reliability and theoretically specified associations. A single total score is not prespecified. Sample sizes are provisional until model-specific simulation and feasibility review. No participant data have been collected or analysed.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} generative artificial intelligence; music creation; emotion regulation; scale development; ordinal psychometrics

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Expected contribution and competing interpretations}
AI-MRFS is proposed as an episode-specific profile of perceived regulatory functions during active AI-assisted music creation. Its contribution would be to distinguish what the activity served to do from why it was initiated, how it was performed and what subsequently changed. A profile could capture simultaneous or competing functions that a single frequency or benefit score would conceal. This is a measurement hypothesis rather than an established advantage over existing music-regulation instruments. Evidence of distinct, interpretable domains and sufficiently precise scores would be necessary before such a profile could be recommended for research.

Several interpretations of the future dimensionality results remain possible. A correlated multidimensional structure would be consistent with partially distinct perceived functions, whereas a single-factor solution could reflect undifferentiated regulatory engagement, insufficient content differentiation or common response processes. A higher-order solution could capture shared variance without making the domain scores interchangeable. If the proposed domains are poorly separated, examination of item meaning and response processes should precede decisions to merge domains or discard items. A revised structure requires an independent confirmatory sample. Neither a large coefficient omega nor favourable global fit establishes content validity or justifies a total score.

\subsection{Implications of computational development}
The synthetic evidence in Table~\ref{tab:diagnostic-screen} illustrates the distinction between local model identification and practical estimability. Jointly weak loadings and strong factor correlations produced frequent improper solutions even at the larger simulated sample size. Conditional interval coverage among proper fits therefore cannot be interpreted as the unconditional probability of obtaining a usable and accurate result. These findings motivate calibration across plausible measurement structures rather than increasing recruitment targets without first addressing content overlap. They do not demonstrate that the proposed AI-MRFS domains overlap in actual respondents.

Agreement between reference and development estimators in selected moderate scenarios is encouraging for numerical implementation but does not establish equivalence under weak identification. The documented disagreement about admissibility and the limited sensitivity of group-comparability diagnostics warrant further investigation. In particular, nonrejection of a measurement-invariance restriction is not proof of equivalence, and observed group score differences should not be attributed to differences in regulatory functions without adequate measurement evidence. The preliminary simulations are not a substitute for an independently reviewed analysis plan or empirical validation.

\subsection{Limitations of the proposed design}
Retrospective assessment may be influenced by the current emotional state, the salience of the recalled episode and beliefs about one's usual behaviour. Recalling the most recent eligible session reduces discretionary selection of a favourable episode but does not eliminate these biases. Initial motives, attributed functions and recalled subsequent clarity are measured from the same respondent and episode. Their associations may therefore reflect shared reporting processes or retrospective reconstruction. Adjustment for recalled initial clarity does not identify a causal change in clarity. Repeated rating of the same episode assesses reproducibility of the report rather than stability of an enduring psychological trait.

Online convenience recruitment may overrepresent experienced creators, highly engaged community members and individuals willing to discuss emotional experiences. The independent CFA sample will address reuse of development participants, but not population representativeness or external replication across recruitment settings. Results may depend on language, cultural conventions of emotional expression, generator interfaces and the extent of user control over the creative process. Missing responses may also reflect difficulties applying an item to a particular episode rather than low endorsement. Selection into the optional retest and incomplete responses require separate reporting and sensitivity assessment.

The proposed scale cannot establish therapeutic efficacy, clinical status or the safety of a particular regulatory strategy. Expression, diversion and repeated attention may have different consequences in different contexts. A high score in avoidance or perseverance-related content is not itself a clinical indicator, just as a high expression score does not establish psychological benefit. No diagnostic cutoffs, individual feedback or clinical recommendations follow from the proposed measurement model.

\subsection{Conditions for subsequent research}
The immediate priorities are qualitative evidence from P02, expert assessment and cognitive interviews, followed by refinement and freezing of the candidate items. The analysis implementation, comparator permissions, recruitment feasibility, ethics determination and preregistration must be resolved at the appropriate stage before fieldwork. Simulation-based sample-size selection should use the frozen model and report uncertainty, failure rates and adverse scenarios. If empirical evidence later supports specific domain scores, independent replication, cross-language adaptation and longitudinal designs can investigate generalisability and sensitivity to change. Causal effects of AI-assisted creation require separate designs with appropriate comparison conditions and outcome measures.

\section{Conclusion}
AI-MRFS is a prospective psychometric proposal, not a validated instrument. Its value depends on demonstrating content relevance, interpretable dimensionality, score precision and construct-specific associations in independent samples. The synthetic findings identify methodological risks and guide further development, but provide no evidence of clinical utility or empirical validity.
