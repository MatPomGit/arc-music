\title{Musical versus Visual Externalization: A Prospective Randomized Study of Perceived Event-Specific Self-Understanding}

\date{Prospective protocol, version 0.6.2, 27 November 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Representing a personal event through music may support reflection on how that experience relates to one's sense of self. We propose a two-arm randomized online experiment comparing a private autobiographical text paired with a participant-selected AI-generated instrumental setting against the same text paired with a participant-selected visual setting. Both groups receive equivalent reflection instructions, candidate selection opportunities and time. The primary outcome is self-reported understanding of the relation between the selected event and the self seven days later, adjusted for baseline understanding. Immediate understanding and current perceived perspective are secondary outcomes. The contrast estimates the effect of the assigned representational package, not the isolated effect of AI, music, or a clinical intervention. No participant data are available. Initial sample-size estimates and a prospective simulation plan are provided.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} narrative identity; autobiographical reasoning; music creation; generative AI; self-event connection; psychological distance.
\section{Background}
Narrative identity integrates reconstructed past and imagined future within an evolving life story \citep{mcadams2013}. Self-event connections can describe continuity, change, or the rejection of a proposed connection; stronger identity centrality is not inherently beneficial \citep{pasupathi2007}. Narrative coherence has distinguishable contextual, chronological and thematic dimensions \citep{reese2011}; a subjective rating of understanding is not a coded narrative-coherence measure. Music can cue autobiographical memories, and familiar songs have been associated with activity in autobiographical memory networks \citep{janata2009}. This evidence concerns retrieval during listening, not experimentally established changes in identity after AI-assisted creation. A newly paired instrumental artefact may provide a symbolic cue for an already recalled event, without necessarily changing factual memory or global identity.
\par The AIM-ER framework (AI-assisted Music for Emotion Regulation) motivates externalisation and alternative perspectives as possible mechanisms. P09 focuses on event-specific autobiographical understanding at a delayed assessment, not P01's goal-affordance interaction, P04's emotional clarity under semantic scaffolding, P07's short-horizon affect, or P08's ownership. No claim is made that this is the first study of musical identity or songwriting. Distinguish understanding, emotional intensity, personal resonance, and accuracy. A person may understand why an event does not define them; forced positive reinterpretation or redemption is inappropriate.
\subsection{Autobiographical reasoning and symbolic representation}
Autobiographical reasoning involves relating experiences to broader self-knowledge rather than merely retrieving a memory \citep{habermas2000,pasupathi2007}. The distinction is important because P09 asks whether a participant can make sense of one already selected event. An improvement in this proximal judgement would be compatible with greater autobiographical understanding but would not demonstrate change in the organisation of the life narrative. Externalisation is defined here as pairing private text with an independently perceptible symbolic artefact, not as disclosure, narrative therapy or the creation of a new autobiographical memory. Both conditions externalise the event in this operational sense. The question is whether one representational package offers an additional advantage over the other. Identity-oriented therapeutic songwriting is a direct conceptual precedent, not evidence that the present procedure improves identity. In a qualitative neurorehabilitation study, \citet{baker2018meaning} examined songs and later interview accounts of 15 people with acquired brain or spinal cord injury following therapist-facilitated songwriting. Participants' accounts described distinct patterns of making meaning and negotiating self-concept. The study did not estimate a randomized effect. In a separate pilot randomized trial of 47 people with brain or spinal cord injury, \citet{baker2019trial} compared 12 identity-focused songwriting sessions against usual care. The primary self-concept difference was not statistically detected ($p=.38$, $d=.44$); a secondary life-satisfaction result favored songwriting at postintervention. This does not establish a global identity benefit or equivalence on self-concept. The trial's abstract and metadata were accessible, but its full text was restricted at the time of this audit. The clinical population, therapist-led repeated composition, usual-care comparison and global self-concept endpoint differ from P09's nonclinical, brief, music-versus-visual representation task and delayed perceived understanding of one event. The reported standardized difference is not used in P09's power calculations.
\par Music may provide a temporal and affective context for reflection. In a comparison of familiar music and famous faces, music-cued memories contained more internal and perceptual detail \citep{belfi2016}. However, those retrieval cues had prior associations, whereas the P09 stimuli are newly selected to accompany an already recalled event. Vivid retrieval and self-understanding are separate constructs, and a newly established association could either aid reflection or distract from it. The visual comparator is therefore theoretically active, not a placebo: a nonfigurative image may also support symbolic elaboration without making an event more vivid. Neither modality is presumed universally superior.
\par Work on self-distancing suggests that adopting another perspective during reflection can facilitate reinterpretation of negative experiences \citep{kross2011}. The analogy motivates H3, but P09 does not explicitly manipulate observer perspective and uses ordinary non-traumatic events. Current perceived broader perspective may be associated with understanding, avoidance or disengagement. It is not a validated measure of self-distancing and does not establish a mechanism by itself.
\subsection{Agency, attribution and competing mechanisms}
Selecting a pre-generated artefact is a constrained form of expressive choice, not full musical composition. In a recent experimental study of AI-assisted music creation, greater automation was accompanied by reduced experienced agency, ownership and task load \citep{he2026}. Such findings motivate measuring perceived contribution and ownership as competing explanations, but concern a different manipulation and outcome. In music students, disclosure of AI authorship changed aesthetic judgements of AI-composed music \citep{qin2026}. This suggests that expectations about the origin of the stimulus can affect evaluation independently of its acoustic features. P09 cannot identify an isolated effect of AI authorship because provenance and modality are not independently randomised. Disclose provenance accurately in both arms using matched neutral language and record prior beliefs and modality preferences before assignment. Do not conceal or misrepresent the origin of the stimuli.
\par The hypothesised pathway links assignment to perceived representational fit, reflective elaboration and subsequent event-specific understanding. Competing paths involve sensory engagement, novelty, expectancy, perceived contribution, positive affect, or distraction. Re-engagement between assessments may sustain an initial association, but is postassignment behaviour. Baseline event importance, baseline understanding and musical experience may predict later ratings and engagement. Randomisation identifies the package contrast under the stated outcome and missingness assumptions; it does not randomise these intermediate variables. Associations with postassignment measures are exploratory and cannot identify causal mediation. A positive H1 without a difference in representational fit, or a difference explained by unequal exposure or differential expectancy, would warrant a narrower interpretation rather than a mechanistic claim.
\par Distinguish P09 from P01, which concerns goal-affordance interactions and emotion regulation, P02, which investigates creators' lived experiences qualitatively, and P08, which manipulates contribution and agency in a factorial design. P09 neither substitutes a delayed self-understanding score for emotional regulation nor treats agency as its primary outcome. Its narrower contribution is a randomised comparison of two symbolic representations for the same privately recalled event, with delayed event-specific measurement. The targeted literature search is not sufficient to establish priority or exhaust the relevant songwriting and autobiographical-memory literature.
\section{Research question and hypotheses}
\begin{enumerate}[label=H\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item Primary: At day 7, event-specific self-understanding is higher after assignment to musical rather than visual externalisation, adjusted for baseline.
\item Secondary: Musical externalisation increases immediate event-specific self-understanding.
\item Secondary: Musical externalisation changes immediate psychological distance from the event, tested two-sided without assuming that greater distance is invariably beneficial.
\end{enumerate}
All tests are two-sided. The secondary family uses Holm correction. A precise null or reversed H1 constrains the proposed advantage. Event importance, change versus continuity, perceived resonance, distress, liking and re-engagement are exploratory; no causal mediation or global identity change is inferred.
\section{Method}
Recruit consenting Polish-speaking adults aged at least 18 who pass a browser/audio check. No musical expertise or AI subscription is required. Participants choose one ordinary, personally meaningful non-traumatic event (e.g. completing a project, learning a skill, or an everyday transition) from the previous 12 months. A fictional substitute is not eligible for the autobiographical estimand. Participants unable or unwilling to select a suitable event may exit before randomisation. Avoid distress induction and invitations to disclose trauma, diagnoses or third-party information.
\par Before randomisation, participants privately compose a short account of the event (three to six sentences) and consider what it does or does not reveal about their values, continuity or change. No text is uploaded or retained by the study; an offline or ephemeral local text field is proposed, subject to security review. Participants choose a private reminder kept locally if desired, and receive a neutral study identifier. At baseline collect event-specific understanding, psychological distance, distress, importance, musical experience and prior AI-music use. The identity of the event is not disclosed.
\par Concealed 1:1 server allocation follows baseline, stratified by formal musical training (at least one year versus less) with concealed variable blocks of 4 and 6. Both groups spend eight minutes constructing a representation and then receive matched reflection questions. In the music condition the unchanged text is paired with an instrumental excerpt chosen from a frozen bank of six AI-generated pieces. In the active visual condition the same text is paired with one of six pre-generated nonfigurative visual designs. Both groups preview, select and revise the pairing within the same time budget; preserve text content. The comparator controls symbolic selection and reflection opportunity, but not sensory modality, expectation, enjoyment or all attentional differences. This is a bounded selection-and-pairing task, not live personalised songwriting. An offline transactional allocation prototype implements concealed blocks within training strata, immutable bank binding, idempotent retries and full-cohort reconciliation. The shared eight-minute task contract has an offline state machine and a manifest validator for six candidates per modality. These validate assignment and file integrity, not stimulus rights, media equivalence or production security. An authenticated service, approved stimuli, local-only interface and withdrawal retention policy remain necessary before recruitment. All materials require provenance, rights and pilot review; neither personal text nor personal prompts are transmitted to a generator. Participants are not blinded to the sensory modality; neutral framing and identical assessment mitigate demand effects. Analysts use masked assignment labels until the analysis is frozen.
\par T0 precedes allocation; T1 follows the task within 30 minutes of randomisation; T2 is scheduled seven days after randomisation, accepted from 144 to 192 hours. Both groups receive identical neutral reminders referring to the original event, without replay or representation at follow-up. Later engagement is recorded, not prohibited or adjusted for in the primary model. Participants confirm whether the follow-up concerns the original event. Unknown or different event and out-of-window observations are missing for the corresponding endpoint. No assessment is assigned zero for incomplete creation. Offer postassignment ratings after early stopping if still consented. Report distress, technical interruptions and completion separately.
\subsection{Measures}
The proposed primary score is the mean of three original 0--10 ratings of understanding how the selected event relates to current self, values and life story; all three are required. Wording permits continuity, change and a conclusion that the event is not self-defining. These candidate ratings are not a validated narrative identity scale. Use the same items at each assessment. A revised candidate bank replaces the first double-barrelled item with one self-event relation question and places permission to endorse absence of connection in the shared instruction. It changes the distance item from general ability to current perceived perspective. These revisions require cognitive review, convergent/discriminant evidence and separate responsiveness testing before instrument freeze. Psychological distance is a separate original 0--10 rating of current perceived broader perspective on the event, distinct from avoidance. Record distress, importance and resonance separately; no inference about factual accuracy, objective coherence, memory improvement or diagnosis is possible without additional measurement. Cognitive interviews and a separate 30--50-person pilot assess comprehension, burden, ceiling effects, within-score consistency and distinction from event centrality, current affect and liking. Revise before confirmation; no item selection based on experimental significance.

\subsection{Prospective content validation and manipulation fidelity}
Before the confirmatory study, define the three ratings as candidate indicators of perceived event-specific understanding across self-description, values and life-story placement. They may form a composite of complementary content rather than interchangeable manifestations of a single latent variable. Following the relevance, comprehensiveness and comprehensibility principles of \citet{terwee2018}, adapted to a non-clinical experimental measure, obtain independent ratings from six to eight experts and conduct two iterative rounds of cognitive interviews with approximately five to eight eligible adults each. Ask participants to paraphrase each item and explain response choices using neutral vignettes or abstract examples, without recording personal narratives. In particular, verify that a well-understood absence of a self-event connection can receive a high understanding rating. Document contradictory interpretations and unresolved content gaps. A revised version must undergo further interviews before freezing; the present wording is provisional.
\par A separate pilot of 30--50 adults will examine completion, day-7 retention, response distributions, item dependence, acceptability and distinction from event centrality, current affect, perceived fit and liking. Report uncertainty rather than applying a single reliability cutoff. A three-item one-factor model with free loadings and residual variances is just identified under conventional continuous covariance modelling, so global fit cannot establish unidimensionality. The pilot cannot support definitive ordinal factor structure, longitudinal measurement invariance or differential item functioning. Report item-level profiles and sensitivity of the composite without choosing items by the treatment effect. Day-7 change may reflect genuine change, changed interpretation or response shift. The single distance item cannot support internal-consistency estimation. These limitations apply even if the primary contrast is statistically significant.
\par Record rendering and audio capability, exposure duration, selection completion and neutral task comprehension separately from perceived fit, effort, novelty, enjoyment, ownership and attribution. Measure immediate outcomes before exploratory manipulation ratings to reduce priming. Do not exclude randomised participants on the basis of fidelity or subjective engagement; report these measures as process diagnostics. Follow-up asks about the original event before the endpoint items, without replay or displaying the selected artefact; record later engagement only after the outcome. Candidate decision rules for content review, fidelity and follow-up feasibility are documented in the prospective validation protocol and require team approval before recruitment.
\section{Statistical analysis}
The target is the mean assignment contrast among eligible randomised adults. Use baseline-adjusted regression with robust HC3 covariance:
\[
Y_{i,7}=\alpha+\beta Z_i+\gamma Y_{i,0}+\eta I_{i,0}+\theta T_i+\lambda U_i+\epsilon_i,
\]
where $Z$ is assigned music versus visual, $I$ baseline event importance, $T$ musical training stratum and $U$ prior use days. H2 substitutes immediate understanding; H3 substitutes immediate psychological distance and baseline distance. Report coefficients in original scale points, 95\% intervals and two-sided tests; no adjustment for postassignment variables. Report all randomised denominators and separate timing, event mismatch and item missingness. Complete-outcome analysis is initial, not guaranteed identification of the full randomised estimand. Report bounded missing-score marginal contrasts and prespecified conditional-mean shift diagnostics for both groups. A separate sensitivity analysis targets the marginal assignment contrast using arm-specific inverse probability of observation weighting (Hajek normalisation). The observation models include both baseline endpoints, event importance, musical training and prior use; logistic slopes receive a fixed weak ridge penalty of 0.1 on prescaled covariates. Under conditional MAR, positivity and a sufficiently accurate response model, weighting can recover the marginal contrast. No postassignment covariates or automatic weight truncation are used. Report minimum fitted response probability, maximum observed weight and effective sample size by arm; probabilities below .05 or effective size below half the observed sample flag instability. For MNAR sensitivity, separately fit arm-specific observed-outcome regressions with the same baseline predictors, then replace missing defined outcomes by conditional means shifted by independently varied arm-specific values from minus two to plus two scale points and clipped to the scale bounds. The 25 marginal contrasts are scenario estimates, not identified MNAR effects. A participant bootstrap stratified by assignment and training refits both response and outcome models, with 999 draws and pointwise percentile intervals; withhold an interval if more than 5\% of its fits fail. These intervals are sensitivity diagnostics, not a simultaneous confidence region, and require calibration before confirmatory use. A repeated-sample screening design evaluates bootstrap IPW and the zero-shift model across twelve hypothesis, missingness and effect configurations, at 588 randomised participants, using 30 independent datasets and 99 refitted bootstrap draws per dataset. Report conditional and operational coverage, bias, RMSE, interval failure, zero exclusion, width and Monte Carlo uncertainty. For MNAR, coverage against the complete-data generating contrast is a stress diagnostic rather than a nominal guarantee; the range across all 25 shift estimates is descriptive, not a confidence set. The small screening replication counts are deliberately insufficient for definitive coverage decisions; the proposed 999-draw analysis still requires higher-precision calibration.
\par Distinguish a missing rating of the original event from an undefined or unresolved target. Nonresponse, an invalid visit, event mismatch or incomplete representation alone does not establish that the target is undefined; the original event was selected at baseline. A negative or absent self-event connection remains a valid response. Record explicit evidence of target status without collecting narrative text. If any target is genuinely undefined or unresolved, withhold full-cohort numerical bounds, IPW and shifts for that endpoint; report status by arm and label the observed-outcome estimate accordingly. Its nominal test is descriptive only and does not enter the confirmatory Holm family as an available test. Preserve the original family size for remaining available secondary tests. Do not assign arbitrary scores or restrict the causal estimand to postassignment survivors. The resolution rules require ethics and pilot review.
\par A development candidate for full-cohort inference with defined but missing outcomes uses the marginal arm-specific Hajek estimator for all three endpoints, including complete data. A stratified participant bootstrap refits response probabilities; centered absolute bootstrap errors give a symmetric interval and a two-sided plus-one test. Overlap warnings, structural undefinedness, unresolved targets and more than 5\% failed refits withhold candidate tests. The secondary Holm family retains both slots. This is a proposed alternative under conditional MAR and response-model assumptions, not an authorized confirmatory hierarchy; independent calibration of availability, bias, coverage, type-I error, power and familywise error is required before replacing the complete-outcome procedure. MNAR shifts remain sensitivity scenarios.
\par A four-cell independent-seed screen of this actual candidate used 20 datasets per cell and 999 bootstrap draws per endpoint (239,760 successful refits, no failures). With MAR and global null, H1 rejected in 1/20 at $N=440$ and 2/20 at $N=600$. In the $N=440$ partial-null cell with a 0.5-point H3 alternative, Holm-adjusted rejection of H3 was 6/20; false secondary-family rejection was 1/20. At $N=600$ under MNAR and global null, H1 rejected in 2/20 and false secondary-family rejection was 4/20 (Wilson 95\% interval .081--.416). H2 coverage was 17/20. All targets were stipulated defined. These counts are imprecise and do not establish nominal error control, adequate power, or identification under MNAR. The candidate remains unapproved and confirmation with incomplete outcomes remains unavailable.
\par The smallest meaningful contrast remains provisional. For planning, a two-sided balanced independent-groups noncentral $t$ calculation at alpha .05 and 90\% power, with no assumed baseline adjustment benefit, gives 470 complete participants at standardised difference 0.30 (588 randomised under 20\% expected missingness); at 0.35 it gives 346 complete (434 randomised). These are sensitivity calculations, not observed effect estimates or a final recruitment target. They assume equal variance, continuous outcomes and balanced observed groups. A reproducible Monte Carlo calibration uses the production HC3 estimator for all three hypotheses. The generator produces three bounded integer items with a shared beta propensity for understanding and a single bounded rating for distance. The four profiles vary baseline association, ceiling tendency and arm-specific dispersion; expected scores are preserved under dispersion changes. Complete data, MCAR, baseline- and assignment-dependent MAR, and outcome-dependent MNAR are crossed with two randomised sample sizes (434 and 588). The synthetic alternatives are 0.5 points for H1 and H2 and zero for H3; a global null is also evaluated. These contrasts are generating values, not justified smallest effects of interest. The simulation reports bias, RMSE, coverage, rejection rates, Monte Carlo standard errors, fit failures, observed fractions by arm and Holm false-rejection rates. T1 outcomes share a visit-level observation indicator. The initial grid models endpoint missingness. An additional four-cell raw-record stress experiment crosses the null and alternative with independent or outcome- and arm-dependent timing, event mismatch and item omissions, at 588 participants and 200 datasets per cell. It exercises the production preparation logic, verifies endpoint-specific observation masks and fits all three HC3 models. The simulated original event remains defined, including when a visit assesses a different event. This does not resolve genuine structural undefinedness or implement block randomisation. Results are synthetic and do not establish measurement validity or justify a final recruitment target. The pilot estimates score distribution and follow-up feasibility, not the target effect. Exclude pilot data from confirmation.
\section{Ethics and reproducibility}
Institutional ethics approval, consent, accessibility, material rights, local text security and a cessation/deletion procedure precede recruitment. No clinical enrichment, personal narrative uploads, psychotherapy claims or paid generation. Provide voluntary skip/exit, neutral grounding and appropriate support information for distress. Do not retain participant records in Git; use approved institutional storage. Freeze instruments, materials, assignment, sample target and analysis before confirmation. Independent scientific review remains pending.
\section{Results}
No participants have been recruited and no empirical results are available.
\subsection{Synthetic calibration evidence}
The archived CI artifact from commit \texttt{134951e} (run 45983137386) contains the 64-cell HC3 grid, bootstrap screening and raw-record stress experiment. Across all three hypotheses and 200 replications per cell, HC3 coverage ranged from .915 to .985 with complete data, .915 to .975 under MCAR, .925 to .980 under the simulated MAR mechanism and .210 to .975 under MNAR. Maximum absolute bias across each mechanism was .019, .028, .028 and .467 points, respectively (rounded); no HC3 fits failed in this grid. The severe MNAR undercoverage shows that computational convergence is not an identification guarantee. The 12 bootstrap cells yielded available intervals in all 360 datasets for each of IPW and zero-shift estimation, with conditional coverage ranging from .867 to 1.000 for each method. With only 30 outer replications per cell, even a rate of .90 has Monte Carlo standard error near .055; these results are screening evidence only. The raw-record grid showed coverage .925 to .980 and maximum absolute bias .036 across its four scenarios and three endpoints. No final recruitment size or confirmatory inference procedure is approved on these results. The archived results predate the allocation prototype and are not validation of the latest commit. The adverse cells were concentrated in the heteroskedastic MNAR profile: at 588 participants and the null, H1 coverage was .215 and H2 coverage .210. A targeted follow-up uses independent seeds, 2,000 datasets per cell in eight heteroskedastic MAR/MNAR configurations (434 or 588 participants, null or alternative) and production 999-draw bootstrap intervals in 1,000 datasets each for the H1 null under MAR and MNAR at 588 participants. The bootstrap jobs are partitioned into ten disjoint shards per mechanism with exact-commit and completeness checks. These are prospective computational checks, not completed findings or a sample-size decision.


\subsection{Rounded sample-size screening}
The proposed 440 and 600 randomised participants correspond to 352 and 480 expected observations at 20\% loss. At standardised differences .35 and .30 respectively, the balanced independent-groups noncentral-$t$ powers are .90563 and .90654. An independent local reproduction of the 64-cell bounded-rating HC3 grid at the new sample sizes used 150 replications per cell (9,600 datasets), four outcome profiles, complete/MCAR/MAR/MNAR observation mechanisms and the original global-null and alternative configurations. H1 rejection at the .5-point alternative under MAR ranged from .700 to .973 at 440 and .800 to .993 at 600 across profiles. Minimum pointwise coverage across H1--H3 and both effect configurations under MNAR was .313 at 440 and .247 at 600; maximum absolute biases were .477 and .451 points. High MNAR rejection rates do not indicate valid power. These low-replication screening results are uncertain and do not resolve missingness, item/timing invalidation, structural undefinedness or bootstrap calibration. Final sample selection remains contingent on a justified smallest effect and pilot evidence. The exact-source CI grid (run 45988878791 at \texttt{dbb0f17}) used 500 replications per cell (32,000 datasets), with no failed fits. H1 alternative rejection under MAR ranged .670--.982 at 440 and .812--.998 at 600. The minimum pointwise MNAR coverage across all hypotheses and configurations was .386 and .256, and the maximum absolute biases were .456 and .444 points, respectively. At 600, global-null H1 rejection reached .744 under MNAR. Larger samples can yield more precise biased estimates; the results support 600 as a planning candidate only under the stated assumptions. Exact-source stratified-block bootstrap screening (run 45990267481 at \texttt{0180814}; 40 datasets and 199 draws per cell) under a heteroskedastic H1 null yielded IPW coverage 40/40 at 440 and 31/40 at 600 under MAR; zero-shift coverage was 40/40 and 33/40. At 600 under MNAR both intervals covered zero in 12/40 datasets, with IPW bias .490. Under the .5-point alternative at 600, IPW coverage was 37/40 under MAR and 16/40 under MNAR. All intervals were returned. The Wilson interval for the 31/40 MAR coverage is approximately [.625,.877]; the small outer count warrants independent higher-precision replication, not automatic acceptance of the bootstrap estimator. Raw-record checks used a separate DGP; matched MAR/MNAR runs reused identical raw-record draws and are counted once.

\section{Discussion}
Musical representation could support an understanding of an event through a new symbolic association, but increased engagement, preference or novelty may also explain a difference. The active visual comparator prevents attributing all benefits of reflection to music, yet cannot isolate music from other modality differences or generative AI from non-AI alternatives. The delayed event-specific endpoint does not establish a reconstructed life story or stable identity change. No neurophysiological or therapeutic claim is warranted. The expanded bibliography remains targeted; a full-text audit and an updated search for directly comparable experiments are still required.
