\title{Session-Specific Avoidance, Brooding and Subsequent Activity Interference in AI-Assisted Music Creation}

\date{Prospective research protocol, version 0.19.0, 28 November 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Generative music systems enable repeated externalisation and revision of emotionally salient narratives. Such activity may support reflection and emotional expression, but immediate relief need not imply improved functioning. We propose a prospective observational diary study of adult AI-assisted music creators to examine whether session-specific avoidance motives are associated with subsequent interference in a previously identified valued activity. The primary estimand is the adjusted within-person association between pre-session avoidance motivation and interference during the first 24 hours after session end. Immediate relief and brooding are secondary variables; perceived insight and creative operations are exploratory. Regulatory flexibility provides a theoretical framework rather than a directly measured construct. The design distinguishes musical sadness from maladaptive functioning, separates within-person fluctuations from stable individual differences, and explicitly addresses selective session reporting and missing follow-ups. The protocol is prospective; no empirical outcomes are reported and no causal or clinical efficacy claim is made.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} generative AI; music creation; experiential avoidance; brooding; emotion regulation; daily diary; functioning.
\section{Introduction}
Generative music systems can lower technical barriers to producing personally meaningful songs. Prompting, writing lyrics, selecting among candidates and listening back constitute distinguishable activities. Their psychological consequences may vary even within the same individual. Evidence from 13 teams (61 participants) in an AI songwriting competition describes repeated sampling and curation as practical responses to limited controllability, not as evidence of rumination \citep{huang2020}. Interviews with 11 non-professional Chinese AI music creators describe situated strategies for negotiating authorship and agency \citep{zhao2026}. Both studies directly concern creative practice but neither establishes effects on next-day functioning or clinical symptoms. The AIM-ER framework (AI-assisted Music for Emotion Regulation) treats regulation as a sequence of potentially adaptive and maladaptive processes rather than an inherent property of AI-generated music. The present study focuses on functional costs, distinct from the delayed-affect hypothesis H6 of MSC-MUS-P01.
\subsection{Emotion regulation as a sequence}
The extended process model distinguishes recognition of a regulatory need, selection of a strategy and its implementation \citep{gross2015}. For the present protocol, starting a creative session is a potential strategy selection, whereas editing prompts, lyrics or generated audio represents implementation. The motive for selection, the emotional response during implementation and subsequent activity interference are distinct observations. An improvement in immediate affect does not establish that the strategy met other personal goals. Conversely, sustained negative affect does not necessarily indicate regulatory failure when emotional engagement serves a valued purpose. The diary captures selected temporal markers rather than all stages of the extended process model.

\subsection{Rumination and reflection}
Brooding is conceptually distinct from reflective pondering \citep{treynor2003}. Repetitive engagement with a personally salient lyric could represent either purposeful elaboration or perseverative thought. The number of generated tracks, negative valence, or repeated listening alone is insufficient to identify either process. Studies of responses to sad music provide relevant but indirect evidence \citep{garrido2013,garrido2015}; these findings do not establish effects of AI-assisted creation. In a questionnaire study of 123 participants, with neuroimaging in a subset of 56, habitual discharge-oriented listening was associated with indicators of maladaptive regulation; the correlational design precludes conclusions about the direction of effects or neural causation \citep{carlson2015}. Conversely, survey data indicate that sad music can be experienced as rewarding and supportive of regulation \citep{taruffi2014}, and retrospective reports link self-selected sad music with acceptance-oriented coping \citep{tol2016}. These findings support competing explanations rather than a general claim of harm. The present study therefore distinguishes negative musical content, regulatory motives, momentary affect and later functional interference.
A review of repetitive thought distinguishes its constructive and unconstructive forms according to content, context and the level of abstraction of processing \citep{watkins2008}. In AI-assisted songwriting, revising a lyric to clarify a specific experience may therefore differ from repeatedly generating variations that leave an unresolved concern unchanged. This distinction is a theoretical application, not an established effect of generative systems. The proposed brooding indicator assesses perceived repetition without progress; it cannot establish the mode of cognitive processing or classify a creative session as pathological. Perceived insight is likewise not equivalent to reflective pondering.

\subsection{Avoidance and regulatory flexibility}
Experiential avoidance concerns attempts to escape or control unwanted internal experiences, potentially at a functional cost \citep{gamez2014}. Temporary distraction may be useful when it permits later re-engagement. Regulatory flexibility instead emphasises sensitivity to context, a repertoire of responses and responsiveness to feedback \citep{bonanno2013}. The Healthy-Unhealthy Music Scale distinguishes patterns of music use \citep{saarikallio2015}, but its original validation in young people and listening-oriented content do not justify direct transfer to adult generative music creation. No unvalidated adaptation will be presented as a validated measure.
A functional account of experiential avoidance focuses on efforts to alter or escape unwanted internal experiences and the consequences of those efforts \citep{hayes1996}. Immediate relief may hypothetically reinforce repeated selection of a strategy even when it competes with a planned activity. However, the same temporary disengagement can facilitate recovery and subsequent action. The pre-session rating captures an avoidance motive rather than a demonstrated avoidance process or a clinical trait. Establishing reinforcement would require evidence about changes in later strategy selection; neither the primary coefficient nor the secondary associations establish such a mechanism.

\subsection{Proposed mechanism}
A pre-existing stressor may influence distress, avoidance motives and subsequent functioning. Avoidance-motivated creation may coincide with immediate relief and, in some sessions, postponed valued actions. Alternatively, relief may enable re-engagement. These competing patterns motivate a functional outcome rather than a classification of creators as healthy or unhealthy. Generation count is ambiguous and may reflect technical failures, exploration or artistic investment. Platform constraints, available time and pre-existing obligations are relevant alternative explanations.
\section{Research questions and hypotheses}
The main research question is whether a stronger motivation to avoid unwanted thoughts or feelings before a naturally initiated creative session is associated with greater interference in an already intended valued activity during the first 24 hours after session end. The unit of observation is the eligible session, nested within the individual. The analysis addresses variation across sessions of the same creator rather than differences between creators.

\subsection{Conceptual model}
The model distinguishes three temporal components: the pre-session motive and contextual conditions, the reported emotional and cognitive experience of the session, and subsequent functioning. Pre-session distress, expected activity difficulty and study day are measured predictors of the primary model. Relief and brooding are recorded after exposure measurement and are not primary adjustment variables. Unmeasured time-varying obligations or stressors may jointly influence the motive, session experience and subsequent functioning. Thus temporal ordering supports the proposed association but does not identify a causal pathway. The target refers to naturally initiated, prospectively logged eligible sessions; missing or independently inapplicable activity outcomes constrain its empirical identification.

\begin{description}
\item[P06-H1 (primary)] Higher pre-session avoidance motivation relative to the person's own mean is associated with greater interference with the same pre-specified valued activity during the first 24 hours after session end, adjusted for pre-session distress, expected difficulty and study day. The directional prediction is $\beta>0$; the prespecified test remains two-sided.
\item[P06-H2 (secondary, non-directional)] Estimate whether immediate relief is associated with subsequent interference and whether this association varies with within-person avoidance motivation. The relief coefficient is evaluated at the participant's mean avoidance in the secondary analysis subset. The interaction estimates variation in the adjusted relief association per point of within-person avoidance. Neither coefficient has a prespecified directional prediction.
\item[P06-H3 (secondary)] Greater post-session brooding relative to the person's own mean is positively associated with subsequent interference, after adjustment for the primary predictors and immediate relief.
\item[P06-E1 (exploratory)] Describe associations involving perceived insight, generation count, duration and activity completion. No directional claim is imposed on generation count. Regulatory flexibility and changes in later strategy selection remain theoretical extensions unless suitable measures are developed and approved.
\end{description}
The primary prediction is specific to functioning, not delayed affect. A sufficiently precise estimate close to zero would constrain the proposed relationship; a negative association would challenge its direction. A non-significant result alone does not demonstrate equivalence or absence of an association. No equivalence margin has been established. H2 comprises two secondary coefficients and H3 comprises one; all three belong to the same prespecified Holm family. H3 concerns a conditional association, not mediation. Secondary analyses require available post-session ratings and can involve different sets of sessions; differences between coefficients across models cannot be attributed to the added predictors alone. See the construct-to-item map for measurement limits.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Design and recruitment}
A 14-day remote prospective diary of naturally occurring AI-assisted music creation among consenting adults aged 18 years or older, recruited from multiple creator communities. Eligibility requires recent AI-assisted music creation and willingness to log naturally initiated sessions. No distress induction, prescribed emotional content, paid generation, or mandatory sharing of lyrics is required. Invite all participants to complete a brief daily check-in reporting the number of naturally initiated creation sessions, including zero and unlogged sessions. A missing check-in is not evidence of non-creation. A separate enrolment calendar enumerates all fourteen planned local dates and the effective date of withdrawal. The primary analysis uses the first eligible session initiated on each local calendar day, selected prospectively rather than by outcome; additional sessions are descriptive. Mobile experience sampling of personal music listening has linked reasons for listening to concurrent affect in 327 participants \citep{randall2017}. The diary design follows the logic of ecological momentary assessment \citep{shiffman2008}, reducing long retrospective recall without eliminating self-report, nonresponse or reactivity biases. A feasibility pilot precedes preregistration and is excluded from confirmatory analysis. The target sample is provisional pending design simulation and pilot completion estimates.
\subsection{Assessment schedule}
At enrolment, record the pseudonymous identifier, first study date and fixed IANA time zone. Age band, language, experience, platform, baseline distress and authorised trait measures are candidate descriptors outside the current analytic export. Collect these only under an approved, separately specified baseline schema; none is required for the primary model. Within 15 minutes before session start, record avoidance motive, distress, expected difficulty of completing an activity and a specific intended action planned for the first 24 hours after session end. Within 15 minutes after the session, record distress, brooding and perceived-insight indicators, duration, number of generations and technical interruptions. At 24 hours after the recorded session end (acceptable window 24--28 hours), assess interference and completion for the fixed first 24 hours after session end, plus current distress and the approved activity-status and missingness reason codes. Intervening events are potential unmeasured contextual influences; no additional event inventory or free-text record is included in the current export. A unique opaque activity identifier must match the pre-session record; mismatched or unconfirmed activities have unknown endpoint applicability. Distinguish an original activity prevented by obstacles (defined outcome, including a missing rating) from an activity rendered independently inapplicable (undefined outcome). Use only a minimal optional reason code, not private text. Unclear status or absent timely assessment leaves applicability unknown; neither undefined nor unknown is coded zero. An assessment before 24 hours is outside the primary window. The narrower 24--26-hour window is prespecified for sensitivity. Record actual start and end timestamps and session identifiers. Missing post-session ratings do not exclude a valid primary observation; they exclude only analyses requiring those ratings. Retrospective pre-session responses are excluded from the primary analysis.

\subsection{Session selection and data integrity}
The prospective first-session designation is recorded before the outcome is known and cannot be reassigned to a later session because a response is missing. For the analytic ledger, a session requires the first-eligible flag, a declared activity with an opaque identifier, all primary predictors, a pre-assessment between zero and 15 minutes before start, and a recorded end strictly after start. Local study dates are checked against the enrolment time zone. Contradictory first-session flags, duplicate identifiers and invalid score values are rejected rather than silently corrected. Post-session ratings outside their window are masked without discarding the primary endpoint. Follow-up applicability is classified independently of the interference rating: a timely, matched and applicable activity has a defined endpoint even if its rating is missing; a timely, matched and independently inapplicable activity has an undefined endpoint; other unresolved cases have unknown applicability. Report these states separately. The current implementation also masks activity completion when interference is unavailable, so completion summaries are restricted to that observed subset unless a separately approved implementation change decouples their validity. The ledger cannot verify unlogged sessions or the truth of self-reports.
\subsection{Operationalisation}
Pre-session avoidance is a proposed single-item 0--10 rating of motivation to avoid unwanted thoughts or feelings; the outcome is a 0--10 rating of interference with a previously named valued action (0 none, 10 complete interference), without attributing interference to music. Sessions without a declared intended action are ineligible for primary estimation but remain in feasibility summaries. Completion status is a secondary behavioural self-report. Immediate relief equals pre-session minus post-session distress (each 0--10). The brooding indicator concerns unwanted repetitive thoughts without progress in understanding; the legacy reflection field measures perceived new insight, not the reflective process. Avoidance is the reported motive of the current session, not a trait. Interference includes contextual barriers and does not establish harm caused by music. The indicators are not diagnostic instruments. The Polish RRS-R is a candidate baseline instrument: an initial validation reported internal consistency of .74 for brooding and .77 for reflection (n=99) \citep{kornacka2016}. The Polish BEAQ validation in a representative sample of 1,216 supported cognitive-emotional and behavioural avoidance dimensions rather than an adequately fitting one-factor model; corresponding internal consistency estimates were .78 and .74 \citep{wardeszkiewicz2024}. Subject to access and scoring verification, retain these subscales separately for descriptive baseline analyses, rather than a presupposed unidimensional score. Neither trait instrument validates the proposed session ratings. No scale score is required for the primary fixed-effects model. HUMS may inform construct mapping, not substitute for the primary endpoint. Novel diary ratings require cognitive interviews, anchor comprehension, floor/ceiling assessment and within-person variation checks.

\subsection{Measurement development and feasibility}
Subject to institutional approval, conduct two iterative rounds of cognitive interviews with approximately five to eight adults per round, followed by a separate 14-day feasibility pilot with 15 to 25 volunteers. Probe the distinction between avoidance motives and deliberate distraction, brooding and purposeful revision, perceived insight and reflective processing, and independently inapplicable activities versus activities prevented by obstacles. Test whether participants can retain the original activity identity and report the fixed observation period. Revise wording on the basis of comprehension and burden, not significance tests. Pilot indicators include the proportion of eligible sessions with a timely matched applicable follow-up and an observed interference score, the median number of eligible sessions per enrolled participant (including zero), score distributions, within-person variation and completion burden. Candidate review thresholds are at least 70\% observed valid follow-ups, a median of at least four eligible sessions and review if more than half of observed ratings occupy one scale endpoint. Report denominators and missingness explicitly; an empty denominator is not a successful feasibility outcome. These are provisional feasibility criteria, not psychometric validation or final sample-size requirements. Freeze the revised diary and analysis plan before confirmatory recruitment.
\section{Statistical analysis}
Let $Y_{it}$ denote interference during the first 24 hours after session end, $A_{it}$ pre-session avoidance, $D_{it}$ initial distress and $K_{it}$ expected difficulty. The primary model uses participant fixed effects to distinguish within-person associations from between-person differences \citep{curran2011}:
\[
Y_{it}=\alpha_i+\beta A_{it}+\gamma D_{it}+\delta K_{it}+\eta t+\varepsilon_{it}.
\]
The coefficient $\beta$ describes the adjusted within-person association per one-point avoidance increase. Day index $t$ denotes the local study day (1 to 14). Report the estimate, participant-cluster robust 95\% confidence interval and two-sided test at .05. Participants with fewer than two eligible complete sessions are omitted from estimation. The implementation requires at least ten contributing participant clusters, positive residual degrees of freedom, full within-person design rank and nondegenerate standard errors. These numerical minima do not establish adequate statistical power. With $G$ retained participants, $N$ sessions and $p$ slope parameters, the cluster sandwich uses the full CR1 factor $[G/(G-1)][(N-1)/(N-G-p)]$ and $t$ inference with $G-1$ degrees of freedom. Report retained participants and sessions alongside the coefficient, standard error, confidence interval and exact two-sided $p$ value; do not report an unavailable estimate as zero. A complete-case estimate is the initial descriptive association among observed defined endpoints. If any eligible session has an undefined or unascertained endpoint, the full-session sensitivity target is not identified and numerical IPW, shifts and bootstrap are withheld pending an approved estimand. Missingness sensitivity is mandatory for inferential interpretation; do not generalise to all sessions. The three archived simulation tables were reproduced at their displayed precision in a separate computational workflow. A subsequent reproduction workflow also completed successfully, including a preliminary bootstrap screen; the exact revision and job identifiers are recorded in the accompanying reproduction documentation. These checks do not establish full bootstrap coverage or validate every numerical claim; current-revision evidence must be verified separately. The tables remain conditional design diagnostics, not a basis for a final sample size without pilot feasibility and further calibration. In a preliminary synthetic calibration (27,000 replications; six sessions and 30\% missingness), the full CR1 correction was conservative under MCAR/MAR (null rejection 2.2--3.2\%). A candidate correction not counting participant effects nested within clusters yielded 4.9--5.6\% but did not remove bias under MNAR. The choice remains provisional pending stress testing. At 160 participants and a latent slope of 0.15, the full correction yielded 95.6\% rejection under MAR; this is a conditional design diagnostic, not a justified final sample target. A separate stress screen of 10,800 synthetic datasets with monotone attrition and heterogeneous residuals gave full-CR1 rejection rates of 62.7, 84.7 and 96.3\% at four, six and ten planned sessions, respectively, for 160 enrollees, a latent slope of 0.15 and 30\% final-session attrition under the simulated MAR-style mechanism. Serial correlation varied jointly with session count; the comparison is not an isolated effect of session count. At 45\% attrition the six-session scenario yielded 77.7\%. Final recruitment targets depend on pilot feasibility and a justified smallest effect. The outcome is bounded, so inspect residuals, floor/ceiling effects and compare an ordinal mixed-model sensitivity if warranted. Fixed effects absorb time-invariant participant characteristics, not time-varying confounding. Immediate relief and post-session brooding are omitted from the primary model because they occur after exposure measurement. For H2, define $L_{it}=D_{it}-D^{\mathrm{post}}_{it}$ and centre $L_{it}$ and $A_{it}$ within each participant using the relief-available complete-case subset. Add centred relief and the product of the two centred variables to the primary predictors. For H3, fit the primary predictors with relief and brooding on the subset with both ratings. Apply Holm adjustment across the fixed family of three coefficients. If a coefficient is unavailable, retain its family slot with an internal $p=1$ for multiplicity correction, but report the coefficient and adjusted $p$ as unavailable with the reason. Report the sample size of each fitted model; the unadjusted coefficient intervals are not simultaneous intervals. Other comparisons are descriptive with uncertainty intervals where available.
\subsection{Missingness, selection and robustness}
Report enrolment, scheduled and active diary days, withdrawal, confirmed non-creation, missing check-ins, contradictions between reported and logged starts, and the lower bound of unlogged sessions. Report eligible starts, completed post-session assessments and valid follow-ups, with reasons where volunteered. Daily denominators quantify selective reporting but do not identify the unobserved avoidance or interference of unlogged sessions. Compare follow-up probability by observed avoidance, initial distress, expected difficulty and day. Only when endpoint applicability is established throughout the eligible target, use a provisional ridge-regularised logistic observation model with the primary pre-session covariates and day, inverse-probability weights with caps 10 and 20, and participant-bootstrap uncertainty with refitting of the observation model; examine positivity. The candidate observation model standardises the four primary predictors and applies a fixed ridge penalty to slopes, not the intercept. Inverse probabilities are computed with a .05 probability floor before weight truncation; report the fraction below that floor and the minimum fitted probability. Conduct deterministic conditional-mean pattern-mixture sensitivity for missing outcomes. For each missing outcome, shift its predicted participant-specific mean by $s+d(A_{it}-\overline A_i)/2$, with $s\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}$ and $d\in\{-1,0,1\}$, and clip to $[0,10]$. Retain observed outcomes unchanged. These 15 scenarios are not multiple imputation. Participants without any observed outcome cannot supply a participant intercept and are excluded from this sensitivity target; report their count. Resample whole participants with replacement, relabel repeated draws as distinct clusters and refit the observation and outcome models. Percentile intervals are withheld unless at least 499 draws are requested and at least 90\% of draws yield jointly valid estimates for both weight caps and all 15 scenarios. Report the number of failed draws. The gate is computational, not evidence of nominal coverage. Report sensitivity to the narrower 24--26-hour timing window. Descriptive subset analyses exclude logged starts in the preceding 24 hours, starts within the fixed outcome period, both, and sessions lacking a confirmed absence of technical failure. Later starts and failures are post-exposure selection variables, so these comparisons do not adjust for confounding or establish causality. Unlogged starts remain unknown. Selection into natural creation and eligible session logging limits the target population; non-creation days are not imputed as sessions. Do not equate missing diary responses with harm. In a separate paired synthetic screening study (2,400 replications), complete-case and IPW estimates were compared with the realized full-data coefficient. At latent slope 0.15 and 30\% missingness, the paired bias under MAR was -0.00171 and -0.00227 (IPW cap 10); under an outcome-dependent MNAR mechanism it was -0.12865 and -0.13996. Thus the specified IPW model is retained as a sensitivity analysis, not a guarantee of correction. The deterministic shift envelope bracketed the full-data coefficient in 98.5--100\% of simulated datasets, but mean widths were 0.308--0.551 slope points. This envelope is not a confidence interval; bootstrap coverage and plausible shift magnitudes remain to be established.
\subsection{Ethics and reproducibility}
This is not a therapeutic intervention. Participants may skip questions or stop without penalty. No clinical diagnosis or risk category is inferred from diary scores. Provide institutionally approved support information and a distress response procedure. The diary is not monitored for emergencies. Recruitment, consent, data handling and withdrawal procedures require institutional approval before cognitive interviews or pilot deployment. Store pseudonymous data in authorised institutional storage with consent and linkage records held separately; do not collect private song texts by default. The candidate cessation rule treats the exact offset-aware withdrawal instant as the first prohibited collection time, including follow-ups of earlier sessions. The first fully inactive local day is a separate calendar field. Before empirical estimation, reconcile the session, enrolment and daily check-in ledgers against the approved cutoff, require timestamps for supplied response payloads, and reject unknown participant identifiers or events preceding enrolment. A failed gate stops estimation rather than silently discarding records. The software does not determine lawful retention or delete records; the university must approve the actual withdrawal and retention policy before data collection. Freeze code, language versions, scoring, sample target and analysis decisions before recruitment of the confirmatory sample. Public release is limited to permissible de-identified or aggregate material and synthetic test data.
\section{Results}
No participant observations or empirical outcomes are reported. The synthetic simulations described in the statistical analysis section are design diagnostics rather than empirical evidence. Future reporting will include attrition, within-person variation, primary and secondary estimates, robustness analyses and protocol deviations, irrespective of the direction of associations.
\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Anticipated contribution and competing interpretations}
The proposed contribution is a prospective within-person examination of the distinction between immediate emotion regulation and subsequent everyday functioning. Unlike an assessment of general attitudes toward generative music, the diary links a reported motive to an activity identified before the session. This temporal separation reduces reliance on retrospective explanations of why creation occurred, but it does not establish that creation or its motive caused subsequent interference. The functional endpoint is defined without reference to the emotional valence of the music.

A positive primary coefficient would be consistent with greater functional interference on occasions when avoidance motivation exceeds the creator's usual level. Competing explanations include anticipation of difficult obligations, concurrent stressors, fatigue and restricted time. A negative coefficient would challenge the directional prediction and could be consistent with temporary disengagement followed by re-engagement; the latter process would still require direct measurement. A coefficient near zero with a narrow interval would constrain the proposed relationship, whereas a wide interval would leave substantial uncertainty. Without a justified equivalence margin, statistical non-significance cannot establish the absence of a meaningful association. Opposing associations in different contexts may also yield a small average slope, although the planned primary model does not identify individual slopes or validate subgroups.

The secondary analyses concern whether immediate relief and later functioning covary and whether their association depends on avoidance motivation. Relief can coexist with interference without demonstrating negative reinforcement, because subsequent strategy selection is not measured as a confirmatory outcome. A positive brooding coefficient would be consistent with a distinction between perseverative thought and purposeful revision, not evidence that repeated generation is itself maladaptive. These analyses condition on post-session reports and may involve different session subsets. Their coefficients cannot be interpreted as direct or indirect causal effects.

\subsection{Measurement, selection and inferential limits}
The proposed single-item indicators measure reported experiences rather than clinical symptoms or validated trait scores. Recall error, social desirability, response reactivity and shared self-report methods may influence both predictors and outcomes. Measurement error need not simply attenuate associations, particularly if it varies by distress or subsequent interference. An opaque activity identifier checks correspondence between records, not whether the activity was objectively performed. The endpoint includes practical barriers unrelated to music. The study therefore cannot establish therapeutic efficacy, clinical harm or the prevalence of a disorder among creators.

The primary analysis conditions on naturally initiated sessions, prospective eligibility, sufficient within-person information and observed defined outcomes. It does not compare creation with abstention and cannot identify the effect of initiating a session. Missed check-ins, unlogged starts and missing outcome ratings represent different sources of selection. Daily denominators improve description of participation but do not recover unobserved motives or functioning. A structurally undefined endpoint is distinct from a missing rating of an applicable activity; unresolved applicability precludes full-session numerical sensitivity in the current contract. Even when all endpoints are defined, inverse-probability weighting and deterministic mean shifts remain conditional on model assumptions. The shift envelope is a range of scenarios, not a confidence interval or a guarantee of correction under MNAR.

Participant fixed effects remove time-invariant differences but not time-varying obligations, stressors or concurrent activities. The hypothetical omitted-variable diagnostic varies the partial $R^2$ of a single unmeasured within-person variable with avoidance and interference over .01, .05, .10 and .20, conditional on the relevant measured predictors. It reports the magnitude and both signs of the corresponding coefficient change for these stipulated strengths. The grid is neither an uncertainty interval nor a bound over all possible confounding structures. Plausible strengths remain to be established. Subset comparisons based on subsequent sessions or technical failures can introduce post-exposure selection and cannot be treated as causal adjustment. Historical synthetic calibration informs the design but does not validate the proposed diary or establish adequate inferential coverage for the eventual empirical sample.

\subsection{Scope and further research}
P06 is related to P01 H6 but is not its exact test: interference with a valued activity during the first 24 hours differs from delayed distress relief, and the brooding-like state indicator is not interchangeable with a validated rumination measure. The distinct functioning endpoint and estimand must remain nonredundant. If data are shared, disclose overlap and assess whether separate publication is scientifically justified. Generalisation is restricted by self-selection, language, the observed platforms, the diary duration and the distribution of eligible sessions. The protocol is not a systematic review; direct AI co-creation evidence and transferred evidence from music listening, psychometrics and longitudinal methodology require a broader reproducible search and full-text audit. Neural mechanisms are not directly measured.

Before confirmatory recruitment, cognitive interviews and feasibility testing should establish whether participants can distinguish avoidance, brooding, insight, immediate relief and practical interference. The team must resolve endpoint applicability, obtain the necessary approvals and instrument permissions, justify the smallest effect of interest, complete statistical calibration and freeze the protocol. A later study could investigate strategy selection over longer periods or compare approved alternative strategies under controlled conditions; those designs are not part of the present observational protocol. No recommendation to restrict or prescribe AI-assisted music creation follows from this prospective work alone.
