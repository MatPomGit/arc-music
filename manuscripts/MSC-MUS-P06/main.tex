\title{Rumination and Maladaptive AI Music Creation: Short-Term Relief, Avoidance, and Subsequent Functioning}
\author{Mateusz Pomianek}
\date{Prospective research protocol, version 0.8.0, 26 November 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Generative music systems enable repeated externalisation and revision of emotionally salient narratives. Such activity may support reflection and emotional expression, but immediate relief need not imply improved functioning. We propose a prospective observational diary study of adult AI-assisted music creators to examine whether session-specific avoidance motives are associated with subsequent interference in a previously identified valued activity. The primary estimand is a within-person adjusted association between pre-session avoidance and next-day interference. Brooding, immediate relief, repeated generation and regulatory flexibility are secondary or exploratory constructs. The design distinguishes musical sadness from maladaptive functioning, separates within-person fluctuations from stable individual differences, and explicitly addresses selective session reporting and missing follow-ups. No empirical data have been collected and no causal or clinical efficacy claim is made.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} generative AI; music creation; experiential avoidance; brooding; emotion regulation; daily diary; functioning.
\section{Introduction}
Generative music lowers technical barriers to the rapid production of personally meaningful songs. Prompting, writing lyrics, selecting among candidates and listening back constitute distinguishable activities. Their psychological consequences may vary even within the same individual. Evidence from 13 teams (61 participants) in an AI songwriting competition describes repeated sampling and curation as practical responses to limited controllability, not as evidence of rumination \citep{huang2020}. Interviews with 11 non-professional Chinese AI music creators describe situated strategies for negotiating authorship and agency \citep{zhao2026}. Both studies directly concern creative practice but neither establishes effects on next-day functioning or clinical symptoms. The AIM-ER framework (AI-assisted Music for Emotion Regulation) treats regulation as a sequence of potentially adaptive and maladaptive processes rather than an inherent property of AI-generated music. The present study focuses on functional costs, distinct from the delayed-affect hypothesis H6 of MSC-MUS-P01.
\subsection{Rumination and reflection}
Brooding is conceptually distinct from reflective pondering \citep{treynor2003}. Repetitive engagement with a personally salient lyric could represent either purposeful elaboration or perseverative thought. The number of generated tracks, negative valence, or repeated listening alone is insufficient to identify either process. Studies of responses to sad music provide relevant but indirect evidence \citep{garrido2013,garrido2015}; these findings do not establish effects of AI-assisted creation.
\subsection{Avoidance and regulatory flexibility}
Experiential avoidance concerns attempts to escape or control unwanted internal experiences, potentially at a functional cost \citep{gamez2014}. Temporary distraction may be useful when it permits later re-engagement. Regulatory flexibility instead emphasizes sensitivity to context, a repertoire of responses and responsiveness to feedback \citep{bonanno2013}. The Healthy-Unhealthy Music Scale distinguishes patterns of music use \citep{saarikallio2015}, but its original validation in young people and listening-oriented content do not justify direct transfer to adult generative music creation. No unvalidated adaptation will be presented as a validated measure.
\subsection{Proposed mechanism}
A pre-existing stressor may influence distress, avoidance motives and subsequent functioning. Avoidance-motivated creation may coincide with immediate relief and, in some sessions, postponed valued actions. Alternatively, relief may enable re-engagement. These competing patterns motivate a functional outcome rather than a classification of creators as healthy or unhealthy. Generation count is ambiguous and may reflect technical failures, exploration or artistic investment. Platform constraints, available time and pre-existing obligations are relevant alternative explanations.
\section{Research questions and hypotheses}
\begin{description}
\item[P06-H1 (primary)] Higher pre-session avoidance motivation relative to the person's own mean is associated with higher next-day interference with a pre-specified valued activity, conditional on pre-session distress and expected difficulty.
\item[P06-H2 (secondary)] Greater immediate relief is not necessarily accompanied by lower next-day interference; estimate its adjusted association with interference and its interaction with avoidance motivation.
\item[P06-H3 (secondary)] Post-session brooding is positively associated with next-day interference after adjustment for pre-session predictors and immediate relief.
\item[P06-E1 (exploratory)] Examine reflection, repeated generation, intention--behaviour discrepancies, and flexibility indicators as possible modifiers or correlates. No directional claim is imposed on generation count.
\end{description}
H1 concerns functioning, not delayed relief. A precise null or negative primary association challenges the proposed avoidance-related pathway. H2 is estimated rather than inferred from a non-significant test of relief. H3 is temporally ordered but not causal mediation.
\section{Method}
\subsection{Design and recruitment}
A 14-day remote prospective diary of naturally occurring AI-assisted music creation among consenting adults aged 18 years or older, recruited from multiple creator communities. Eligibility requires recent AI-assisted music creation and willingness to log naturally initiated sessions. No distress induction, prescribed emotional content, paid generation, or mandatory sharing of lyrics is required. Participants may log non-creation days through a brief evening check-in. The primary analysis uses the first eligible session initiated on each local calendar day, selected prospectively rather than by outcome; additional sessions are descriptive. A feasibility pilot precedes preregistration and is excluded from confirmatory analysis. The target sample is provisional pending design simulation and pilot completion estimates.
\subsection{Assessment schedule}
At enrolment, collect age band, language, experience, platform, trait rumination and avoidance if authorized, and relevant baseline distress measures. Within 15 minutes before session start, record avoidance motive, distress, expected difficulty of completing an activity and a specific intended action to be performed within 24 hours. Within 15 minutes after the session, record distress, brooding and reflection indicators, duration, number of generations and technical interruptions. At 24 hours after the recorded session end (acceptable window 20--28 hours), assess interference with the same activity, completion status, current distress and intervening events. Record actual start and end timestamps and session identifiers. Missing post-session ratings do not exclude a valid primary observation; they exclude only analyses requiring those ratings. Retrospective pre-session responses are excluded from the primary analysis.
\subsection{Operationalisation}
Pre-session avoidance is a proposed single-item 0--10 rating of motivation to avoid unwanted thoughts or feelings; the outcome is a 0--10 rating of interference with a previously named valued action (0 none, 10 complete interference), without attributing interference to music. Participants who cannot identify an intended action are not eligible for the primary session estimand but remain in feasibility summaries. Completion status is a secondary behavioural self-report. Immediate relief equals pre-session minus post-session distress (each 0--10). Brooding and reflection indicators are proposed session ratings, not diagnostic instruments. The Polish RRS-R is a candidate baseline instrument: an initial validation reported internal consistency of .74 for brooding and .77 for reflection (n=99) \citep{kornacka2016}. The Polish BEAQ validation in a representative sample of 1,216 supported cognitive-emotional and behavioural avoidance dimensions rather than an adequately fitting one-factor model; corresponding internal consistency estimates were .78 and .74 \citep{wardeszkiewicz2024}. Subject to access and scoring verification, retain these subscales separately for descriptive baseline analyses, rather than a presupposed unidimensional score. Neither trait instrument validates the proposed session ratings. No scale score is required for the primary fixed-effects model. HUMS may inform construct mapping, not substitute for the primary endpoint. Novel diary ratings require cognitive interviews, anchor comprehension, floor/ceiling assessment and within-person variation checks.
\section{Statistical analysis}
Let $Y_{it}$ denote next-day interference, $A_{it}$ pre-session avoidance, $D_{it}$ initial distress and $K_{it}$ expected difficulty. The primary model uses participant fixed effects:
\[
Y_{it}=\alpha_i+\beta A_{it}+\gamma D_{it}+\delta K_{it}+\eta t+\varepsilon_{it}.
\]
The coefficient $\beta$ describes the adjusted within-person association per one-point avoidance increase. Day index $t$ is measured since enrolment. Report the estimate, participant-cluster robust 95\% confidence interval and two-sided test at .05. At least two eligible complete sessions per participant and identifiable within-person variation are required. A complete-case estimate is the initial analysis; missingness sensitivity is mandatory before interpretation. In a preliminary synthetic calibration (27,000 replications; six sessions and 30\% missingness), the full CR1 correction was conservative under MCAR/MAR (null rejection 2.2--3.2\%). A candidate correction not counting participant effects nested within clusters yielded 4.9--5.6\% but did not remove bias under MNAR. The choice remains provisional pending stress testing. At 160 participants and a latent slope of 0.15, the full correction yielded 95.6\% rejection under MAR; this is a conditional design diagnostic, not a justified final sample target. A separate stress screen of 10,800 synthetic datasets with monotone attrition and heterogeneous residuals gave full-CR1 rejection rates of 62.7, 84.7 and 96.3\% at four, six and ten planned sessions, respectively, for 160 enrollees, a latent slope of 0.15 and 30\% final-session attrition under the simulated MAR-style mechanism. Serial correlation varied jointly with session count; the comparison is not an isolated effect of session count. At 45\% attrition the six-session scenario yielded 77.7\%. Final recruitment targets depend on pilot feasibility and a justified smallest effect. The outcome is bounded, so inspect residuals, floor/ceiling effects and compare an ordinal mixed-model sensitivity if warranted. Fixed effects absorb time-invariant participant characteristics, not time-varying confounding. Immediate relief and post-session brooding are omitted from the primary model because they occur after exposure measurement. Secondary models add relief, its interaction with within-person avoidance, and brooding; apply Holm adjustment to the three predeclared secondary coefficients (relief, interaction, brooding). Other comparisons are descriptive with uncertainty intervals.
\subsection{Missingness, selection and robustness}
Report enrollment, diary days, eligible starts, completed post-session assessments and valid follow-ups, with reasons where volunteered. Compare follow-up probability by observed avoidance, initial distress, expected difficulty and day. Use a provisional ridge-regularized logistic observation model with the primary pre-session covariates and day, inverse-probability weights with caps 10 and 20, and participant-bootstrap uncertainty with refitting of the observation model; examine positivity. Conduct deterministic conditional-mean pattern-mixture sensitivity for missing outcomes with shifts of 0, 1 and 2 scale points in either direction and differential shifts proportional to within-person avoidance, bounded to the response scale. Report participant-bootstrap uncertainty; this is not multiple imputation. Report sensitivity to timing windows, technical failures, overlapping follow-up periods and preceding sessions. Selection into natural creation and eligible session logging limits the target population; non-creation days are not imputed as sessions. Do not equate missing diary responses with harm. In a separate paired synthetic screening study (2,400 replications), complete-case and IPW estimates were compared with the realized full-data coefficient. At latent slope 0.15 and 30\% missingness, the paired bias under MAR was -0.00171 and -0.00227 (IPW cap 10); under an outcome-dependent MNAR mechanism it was -0.12865 and -0.13996. Thus the specified IPW model is retained as a sensitivity analysis, not a guarantee of correction. The deterministic shift envelope bracketed the full-data coefficient in 98.5--100\% of simulated datasets, but mean widths were 0.308--0.551 slope points. This envelope is not a confidence interval; bootstrap coverage and plausible shift magnitudes remain to be established.
\subsection{Ethics and reproducibility}
This is not a therapeutic intervention. Participants may skip questions or stop without penalty. No clinical diagnosis or risk category is inferred from diary scores. Provide appropriate support information and an ethics-approved distress procedure. Store pseudonymous data in authorized institutional storage; do not collect private song texts by default. Freeze code, language versions, scoring, sample target and analysis decisions before recruitment of the confirmatory sample. Public release is limited to permissible de-identified or aggregate material and synthetic test data.
\section{Results}
No participants or empirical outcomes are available. The reported synthetic simulations are design diagnostics, not empirical evidence. Report attrition, variation, primary and secondary estimates, robustness and deviations irrespective of direction.
\section{Discussion}
The proposed contribution is a prospective within-person test of the distinction between immediate emotion regulation and subsequent everyday functioning. A positive association would be consistent with, but not prove, avoidance-related interference. A null association, a negative association or high heterogeneity would constrain the theoretical claim. Reverse causation, planned-activity difficulty, selective logging, concurrent stressors and shared self-report measurement remain limitations. The study is distinct from P01 H6 only if its functioning endpoint and analysis remain nonredundant. If data are shared, disclose overlap and assess whether separate publication is scientifically justified. Generalization beyond self-selected creators and the observed platforms is limited. The protocol is not a systematic review; the targeted literature includes direct studies of AI co-creation but requires a broader search and full-text audit before submission.
