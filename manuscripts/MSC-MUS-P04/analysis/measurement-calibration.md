# PAQ-S-PL reliability and measurement-error calibration
Status: PROSPECTIVE. No participant-level observations are included.
The primary moderator is the observed six-item sum. Reliability is estimated on complete baseline item responses using a prespecified ordinal-probit one-factor model with independent item residuals. Marginal thresholds are estimated from empirical cumulative proportions; positive loadings (factor variance fixed to one) minimise off-diagonal Pearson correlation residuals via 41-node Gaussian-Hermite quadrature. This is a limited-information moment fit, not full-information CFA or WLSMV. For item response X_j and factor L, compute conditional means and variances, then omega_categorical = Var(E(sum X_j | L)) / [Var(E(sum X_j | L)) + E(Var(sum X_j | L))]. Under conditional independence the conditional variances add. This estimates model-implied reliability of the observed sum, not a continuous latent response sum. Report model and observed sum variance, residual discrepancies, loadings, category counts, alpha and complete/missing counts. No fit index or goodness-of-fit significance is claimed. Positive boundary solutions, empty response categories, fewer than 100 complete cases and nonconvergence are rejected. Resample participants 1,000 times and report percentile interval only with at least 95% valid fits; report failure counts. These computational rules and the factor model require review; no empirical reliability is asserted. Reference: Flora (2020), https://doi.org/10.1177/2515245920951747. A poor one-factor fit limits the interpretation.

Measurement-error simulation: 12 scenarios (N=640/1000; latent interaction 0/.60 points per latent population SD; independent item noise SD .5/.9/1.4), 300 datasets each. Six ordinal responses with hypothetical thresholds, bounded uniform standardised latent trait and independent random assignment. The conditional composite mean is linear in latent trait; outcome has structural zeros and bounded fit, no missingness. Under this specific model the observed-score population target equals latent interaction times correlation of the standardised latent trait and observed sum; estimate correlation on an independent 200,000-case synthetic reference per noise scenario, report block MCSE. Sample standardisation introduces finite-sample differences; nominal coverage is assessed against population targets, separately for observed and oracle regressions. Oracle uses unavailable latent values and is not an analysis option for participant data. Report bias, RMSE, rejection, coverage, valid and failed counts with MC intervals. No universal disattenuation formula, no correction by validation omega, no assumption of observed-score reliability equal to squared latent-score correlation. The generator is hypothetical and deliberately not fitted to validation or pilot data. Prior missingness calibration is separate; these complete-outcome scenarios cannot establish final power.
