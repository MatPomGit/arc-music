\begin{abstract}
Generative music interfaces can delegate musical decisions and their execution to different degrees. We propose a preregistered online factorial experiment crossing final decision authority (participant versus fixed system rule) with execution of arrangement changes (manual application versus automatic application). Conditions use identical pre-generated instrumental candidates, preference elicitation, listening opportunities and task duration. The primary estimand is the marginal assignment contrast of decision authority on task-specific psychological ownership. Secondary contrasts concern state agency and the authority-by-execution interaction on ownership. This protocol concerns a bounded arrangement task, not unrestricted live generation or legal authorship. No participant data or empirical results are available.
\end{abstract}
\section{Background}
Psychological ownership concerns subjective belongingness rather than legal entitlement. Ownership theory identifies control, knowledge of the target and investment of self as plausible routes to felt ownership \citep{pierce2001,pierce2003}. Felt ownership, perceived influence, attributed authorial contribution and objective available control are distinct constructs. AI-assisted text creation has demonstrated divergence between felt ownership and declared authorship \citep{draxler2024}; transfer to music remains an empirical question. Research on automation and musical expertise in AI music co-creation has already investigated task load, ownership and state agency \citep{he2026}. A simple automation comparison is therefore not a sufficient novelty claim. We independently vary decision authority and execution at fixed candidate availability; the design does not reproduce the earlier serial mediation analysis. A decision-authority effect may include preference congruence, and an execution effect may include effort, motor interaction and waiting. Neither effect isolates a latent psychological mechanism.
\section{Research questions and hypotheses}
\begin{description}
\item[P08-H1 (primary)] Participant decision authority increases psychological ownership relative to a fixed system decision rule, averaged equally over manual and automatic execution.
\item[P08-H2 (secondary)] Participant decision authority increases reported state agency, averaged equally over execution conditions.
\item[P08-H3 (secondary)] The authority-by-execution interaction on psychological ownership differs from zero. Manual application might amplify meaningful control or add little when authority is already present.
\end{description}
All tests are two-sided. Main execution contrasts, simple effects, musical expertise moderation, preference congruence, perceived effort, liking and attributed contribution are exploratory. Postassignment measures are not primary covariates or causal mediators. This extends AIM-ER (AI-assisted Music for Emotion Regulation) H3/H4/H8 by splitting control dimensions and prioritising ownership rather than emotion-regulation outcomes. P04 manipulates semantic assistance, not decision authority.
\section{Method}
\subsection{Participants and materials}
Recruit consenting Polish-speaking adults aged at least 18 who pass a sound and browser check and complete baseline measures. Prior generator use and musical training are measured, not required. Use one fixed non-personal creative brief, a common 30-second instrumental starter sketch, and four successive decision stages (rhythm layer, harmonic texture, instrumentation and ending). Each stage has two compatible pre-generated alternatives; freeze candidate audio, gains, duration, interface, rule and material provenance before pilot. Both alternatives are auditionable in each condition. The system choice is a predetermined balanced rule, independent of participant preference and execution assignment, not a purported live model decision. No live generator, personal content or paid access is required.
\subsection{Randomisation and manipulation}
Concealed server-side randomisation after baseline assigns four cells equally, stratified by prior formal musical training (at least one year versus less) with concealed variable block sizes. The allocation algorithm and seed handling require implementation and review before recruitment. All participants indicate a preference at every stage. Under participant authority the preferred option determines the selected revision; under system authority the predeclared rule determines it transparently. In manual execution the participant applies the determined revision using a button; in automatic execution the interface applies the same revision after a fixed countdown. Stage duration and preview opportunities are matched. No hidden overrides or deceptive attribution. Manual execution is an application action, not an additional musical decision.
\par Four decisions and final playback occur within eight minutes. Record coarse stage events, indicated preference, selected and applied option, timestamps, technical failure and assignment. Initial draft is defined in every condition; ownership concerns the version actually heard, including a partial arrangement if assessable. No unheard draft or unobserved rating is assigned zero. Offer assessment after early stopping if still consented. Pilot whether controls are comprehensible, timing and exposure comparable, and the task feels creative rather than administrative.
\subsection{Measures}
Before assignment: training stratum, prior AI-music use (days in last 30), task familiarity (0--10) and anticipated ownership (0--10) for the neutral described task. Primary outcome: mean of three original 0--10 ownership ratings of the heard arrangement, all required; refer to felt personal belonging, own work and connection to one's creation, not control or legal rights. State agency: mean of three separate original 0--10 ratings of perceived influence over changes, ability to direct outcomes and correspondence of outcome changes to one's decisions. Attributed contribution uses a separate 0--100 human contribution estimate. Check understanding of who determined the revisions and who applied them after primary ratings. Record effort, liking and technical difficulty separately. Original candidate items are not validated scales; cognitive interviews, pilot content validity, inter-item relations, factor separation and reliability uncertainty precede measurement freeze. If overlap is substantial, revise before confirmation rather than selecting items after inspecting effects.
\section{Analysis}
Code authority $A$ and manual execution $E$ as $-0.5,+0.5$. Fit
\[
Y_i=\alpha+\beta_A A_i+\beta_E E_i+\beta_{AE}A_iE_i+\gamma^\top X_i+\epsilon_i.
\]
Use HC3 covariance and two-sided $t$ intervals with residual degrees of freedom. $X$ contains training stratum, prior-use days, familiarity and anticipated ownership; use centered baseline covariates and no postassignment adjustment. $\beta_A$ is the equally weighted marginal assignment contrast under this additive-covariate model; $\beta_{AE}$ is a difference of differences. Report four cell counts, observed means, contrast estimates and 95\% intervals. The target population is all eligible randomised adults; complete-outcome regression is the initial estimate under its missingness assumptions, not proof of identification of the full randomised-population estimand. H2 substitutes agency; H3 uses the interaction on ownership. Holm correction covers H2 and H3; preserve the family when a secondary contrast is unavailable. Report all randomised denominators, missing outcomes, manipulation failures, partial tasks and technical interruptions by assigned cell. No exclusions for unsuccessful manipulation, nonadherence, low liking or insufficient manual operations. Per-protocol and exploratory mediation are not confirmatory.
\par Missing outcomes require bounded sensitivity by assigned cell and baseline-adjusted availability weighting before final inference. An optional outcome remains missing after withdrawal, not zero. Target 90\% primary power at a substantively justified smallest meaningful difference. The normal approximation for a balanced two-sided marginal contrast with standardised difference .30 and alpha .05 gives roughly 468 complete participants, before attrition or covariates. This does not power the interaction and is not a recruitment decision. Simulate bounded composite scores, unequal variances, interactions, nonadherence and MCAR/MAR/MNAR separately. Pilot 30--50 adults after cognitive interviews; exclude pilot records from confirmation.
\section{Ethics and reproducibility}
Consent, ethics approval, approved data controller/storage and audio rights precede pilot. No autobiographical disclosures, clinical claims, false promises of legal ownership or paid tools. Store approved pseudonymous records outside Git; public code and synthetic data only. Freeze materials, randomisation, scoring, missingness and analysis before confirmation. Independent review is pending.
\section{Results}
No participants have been recruited and no empirical data are available.
\section{Discussion}
The factorial design separates selection authority from execution rather than comparing broad automation levels. Preference congruence may partly explain the authority contrast; execution may influence effort and attention. Neither mechanism is identified by the marginal assignment effect alone. A finite candidate bank and transparent rule improve experimental control but limit generalisation to open-ended commercial generation. Self-reported agency does not establish neural or therapeutic effects. The initial bibliography is targeted, not exhaustive.
