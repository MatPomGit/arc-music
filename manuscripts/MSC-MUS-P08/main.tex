\begin{abstract}
Generative music interfaces can delegate musical decisions and their execution to different degrees. We propose a preregistered online factorial experiment crossing final decision authority (participant versus fixed system rule) with execution of arrangement changes (manual application versus automatic application). Conditions use identical pre-generated instrumental candidates, preference elicitation, listening opportunities and task duration. The primary estimand is the marginal assignment contrast of decision authority on task-specific psychological ownership. Secondary contrasts concern state agency and the authority-by-execution interaction on ownership. This protocol concerns a bounded arrangement task, not unrestricted live generation or legal authorship. No participant data or empirical results are available.
\end{abstract}
\section{Background}
Psychological ownership concerns subjective belongingness and is conceptually distinct from legal entitlement. Ownership theory identifies control, knowledge of the target and investment of self as plausible routes to felt ownership \citep{pierce2001,pierce2003}. Felt ownership, perceived influence, attributed authorial contribution and objective available control are distinct constructs. AI-assisted text creation has demonstrated divergence between felt ownership and declared authorship \citep{draxler2024}; transfer to music remains an empirical question. Research on automation and musical expertise in AI music co-creation has already investigated task load, ownership and state agency \citep{he2026}. A simple automation comparison is therefore not a sufficient novelty claim. We independently vary decision authority and execution at fixed candidate availability; the design does not reproduce the earlier serial mediation analysis. A decision-authority effect may include preference congruence, and an execution effect may include effort, motor interaction and waiting. Neither effect isolates a latent psychological mechanism.
\par The division of automation into selection and implementation \citep{parasuraman2000} motivates the factorial manipulation. State agency is a judgement about influence over action outcomes \citep{haggard2017}. Autonomy additionally concerns self-endorsement \citep{deci2000}. A participant can apply a rule-selected change and feel motor involvement while having little influence over which arrangement is heard. Conversely, a participant can choose the revision without touching the apply control. The explicit state rating only tests perceived influence. It does not measure temporal binding or neural control. In a four-week ecological study, professional engagement with an AI variation tool relied on musicians' initial material and interpretation, supporting reported ownership \citep{krol2025}. P08 supplies common source material and controls the role of input originality at the cost of ecological breadth.
\par \citet[Sections 3.2--3.5]{he2026} combine levels of intervention and task involvement across automation conditions, and measure trained and novice groups separately. Their contrasts and proposed serial mechanism motivate tests of distinct dimensions, but do not provide an effect size for this factorial design. Here, preference congruence may compete with the prediction that decision authority matters independently of liking. Differences in preview exposure, feedback latency or role comprehension could contribute to apparent authority or execution effects. Exploration records these candidate explanations without conditioning primary effects on postassignment behaviour.

\section{Research questions and hypotheses}
\begin{description}
\item[P08-H1 (primary)] Participant decision authority increases psychological ownership relative to a fixed system decision rule, averaged equally over manual and automatic execution.
\item[P08-H2 (secondary)] Participant decision authority increases reported state agency, averaged equally over execution conditions.
\item[P08-H3 (secondary)] The authority-by-execution interaction on psychological ownership differs from zero. Manual application might amplify meaningful control or add little when authority is already present.
\end{description}
All tests are two-sided. Main execution contrasts, simple effects, musical expertise moderation, preference congruence, perceived effort, liking and attributed contribution are exploratory. Postassignment measures are not primary covariates or causal mediators. This extends AIM-ER (AI-assisted Music for Emotion Regulation) H3/H4/H8 by splitting control dimensions and prioritising ownership rather than emotion-regulation outcomes. P04 manipulates semantic assistance, not decision authority.
\section{Method}
\subsection{Participants and materials}
Recruit consenting Polish-speaking adults aged at least 18 who pass a sound and browser check and complete baseline measures. Prior generator use and musical training are measured, not required. Use one fixed non-personal creative brief, a common 30-second instrumental starter sketch, and four successive decision stages (rhythm layer, harmonic texture, instrumentation and ending). Each stage has two compatible pre-generated alternatives; freeze candidate audio, gains, duration, interface, rule and material provenance before pilot. Both alternatives are auditionable in each condition. The system choice is a predetermined balanced rule, independent of participant preference and execution assignment, not a purported live model decision. No live generator, personal content or paid access is required.
\subsection{Randomisation and manipulation}
Concealed server-side randomisation after baseline assigns four cells equally, stratified by prior formal musical training (at least one year versus less) with concealed variable block sizes. A transactional prototype uses variable block sizes 4, 8 and 12 within training strata, operating-system randomness and idempotent assignment. Secure server integration, consent and eligibility gates, allocation concealment and independent review remain prerequisites. All participants indicate a preference at every stage. Under participant authority the preferred option determines the selected revision; under system authority the predeclared rule determines it transparently. In manual execution the participant applies the determined revision using a button; in automatic execution the interface queues the same revision. Both conditions apply queued revisions at the same deadline. Failure to confirm manually preserves the latest available arrangement. Stage duration and preview opportunities are matched. No hidden overrides or deceptive attribution. Manual execution is an application action, not an additional musical decision.
\par Four decisions and final playback occur within eight minutes. Record coarse stage events, indicated preference, selected and applied option, timestamps, technical failure and assignment. Initial draft is defined in every condition; ownership concerns the version actually heard, including a partial arrangement if assessable. No unheard draft or unobserved rating is assigned zero. Offer assessment after early stopping if still consented. Pilot whether controls are comprehensible, timing and exposure comparable, and the task feels creative rather than administrative.
\subsection{Measures}
Before assignment: training stratum, prior AI-music use (days in last 30), task familiarity (0--10) and anticipated ownership (0--10) for the neutral described task. Primary outcome: mean of three original 0--10 ownership ratings of the heard arrangement, all required; refer to felt personal belonging, own work and connection to one's creation, not control or legal rights. State agency is a candidate mean of three task-specific 0--10 ratings. A revised item bank proposes experienced action-outcome contribution and contingency rather than factual authority or preference correspondence; expert review must establish separation from manual execution and role comprehension. Attributed contribution uses a separate 0--100 human contribution estimate. Check understanding of who determined the revisions and who applied them after primary ratings. Record effort, liking and technical difficulty separately. Original candidate items are not validated scales; cognitive interviews, pilot content validity, inter-item relations, factor separation and reliability uncertainty precede measurement freeze. If overlap is substantial, revise before confirmation rather than selecting items after inspecting effects.
\subsection{Content validity and factorial fidelity}
The general Sense of Agency Scale measures context-independent beliefs about agency \citep{tapal2017}; it is not interchangeable with the task-specific perceived influence ratings proposed here. Content review follows relevance, comprehensibility and comprehensiveness principles adapted from \citet{terwee2018}. The original third agency item may reflect preference congruence and the second ownership item may invite legal attribution. The revised candidate avoids those explicit referents but may retain redundancy; no item bank is frozen. Independent expert ratings and cognitive interviews must resolve these interpretations before instrument freeze. An isolated three-item factor is just identified under conventional covariance modelling; its fit cannot establish unidimensionality. A small feasibility pilot cannot establish ordinal measurement invariance or discriminant validity. A larger independent validation sample is needed for comparison of correlated two-factor and one-factor models, factor correlation uncertainty and cross-loading assessment.
\par The factorial fidelity plan distinguishes objectively selected and applied options, confirmed hearing, role comprehension, and task and preview duration. Compare timing across all six pairs of assigned cells using Welch 90\% confidence intervals and independently prespecified practical equivalence margins \citep{lakens2017}. A joint equivalence claim requires all intervals to lie inside the respective bounds. A missing timing record, insufficient cell size or degenerate variance precludes such a claim. Pilot estimates may be inconclusive; absence of a significant difference is not evidence of equivalence. Explicitly record unknown responses and report all assigned denominators. Do not condition confirmatory contrasts on fidelity.
\section{Analysis}
Code authority $A$ and manual execution $E$ as $-0.5,+0.5$. Fit
\[
Y_i=\alpha+\beta_A A_i+\beta_E E_i+\beta_{AE}A_iE_i+\gamma^\top X_i+\epsilon_i.
\]
Use HC3 covariance and two-sided $t$ intervals with residual degrees of freedom. $X$ contains training stratum, prior-use days, familiarity and anticipated ownership; use centered baseline covariates and no postassignment adjustment. $\beta_A$ is the equally weighted marginal assignment contrast under this additive-covariate model; $\beta_{AE}$ is a difference of differences. Report four cell counts, observed means, contrast estimates and 95\% intervals. The intended target population is all eligible randomised adults, conditional on the ownership and agency outcomes being well-defined under the assigned task. Complete-outcome regression is an initial estimate under its missingness assumptions, not proof of identification of the full randomised-population estimand. Hearing is postassignment and may differ by cell; neither a complete-case nor a heard-only contrast identifies a common principal stratum. If the full-cohort target cannot be supported, report exposure flow and descriptive estimates without confirmatory testing; redesign or a separately approved estimand is required before claiming a full-cohort effect. H2 substitutes agency; H3 uses the interaction on ownership. Given limited simulated power, exploratory classification of H3 is proposed unless an approved joint calibration supports the intended confirmatory hierarchy; no change to the historical tests is made. Holm correction covers H2 and H3; preserve the family when a secondary contrast is unavailable. Report all randomised denominators, missing outcomes, manipulation failures, partial tasks and technical interruptions by assigned cell. No exclusions for unsuccessful manipulation, nonadherence, low liking or insufficient manual operations. Per-protocol and exploratory mediation are not confirmatory.
\par Missing-outcome diagnostics comprise unadjusted bounded marginal and interaction contrast ranges, ridge-logistic inverse availability weighting using assignment and baseline variables (caps 10 and 20), and 27 bounded deterministic conditional-mean scenarios varying common, authority and interaction shifts. These full-cohort numerical diagnostics are available only when all randomised participants have confirmed hearing of an assessable arrangement, including those with missing item ratings. If any participant did not hear an assessable version or exposure remains unresolved, the full-cohort construct is undefined or unresolved; no numerical bounds, IPW or shift estimates are reported for that cohort. Observed-outcome HC3 estimates and nominal tests are then explicitly descriptive, and the confirmatory Holm entries are unavailable. This conservative gate does not assert that a defined-but-missing rating invalidates confirmation; identification of that contrast still depends on missingness assumptions. Report known hearing, known non-hearing and unresolved exposure by assigned cell. Scenario results are point diagnostics rather than confidence intervals or multiple imputation. Known heard records additionally receive a separate descriptive analysis. Its inverse-probability model weights only missing ratings within the population with confirmed hearing. Known unheard records have no defined target rating and unknown exposure is reported separately. A participant bootstrap within assigned cells refits the observation and outcome models on each draw to quantify sampling uncertainty conditional on each specified scenario. A scenario interval does not capture uncertainty about the choice of shift. Selection into the heard population can be affected by assignment, so these contrasts cannot be interpreted as effects in a common principal stratum. Reject interval reporting when more than 5\% of refits fail. Target 90\% primary power at a substantively justified smallest meaningful difference. The normal approximation for a balanced two-sided marginal contrast with standardised difference .30 and alpha .05 gives roughly 468 complete participants, before attrition or covariates. The proposed rounded planning target is 500 complete observations, not an approved recruitment target. This does not power the interaction. The implemented 36-cell initial simulation crosses 240, 480 and 720 randomised participants, latent marginal authority contrasts 0 and 0.45, interaction contrasts 0 and 0.60, and MCAR/MAR/MNAR outcome loss with 20 percent expected missingness. It generates correlated bounded three-item composites, unequal variances and baseline covariates, using the production HC3 estimator and the secondary Holm correction. Independent large complete-data reference samples distinguish latent inputs from observed-scale contrasts. Report conditional and unconditional rejection, coverage, bias, joint rejection and Monte Carlo uncertainty. The original screen uses 500 replications per cell. Extended calibration uses 2,000 replications per cell with 15\% assigned-feature suppression as a scenario, in addition to the simulated 20\% outcome loss. Analysis retains the assigned cells. Exact nulls arising from symmetry use zero targets. Other cells use independently simulated complete-data contrasts and their standard errors. Report RMSE and contrast-specific failures in addition to bias, coverage and rejection. Final sample planning remains conditional on approved smallest effects and pilot-informed parameters. Pilot 30--50 adults after cognitive interviews; exclude pilot records from confirmation.


\paragraph{Rounded sample-size sensitivity (27 November 2026).}
At 500 complete observations, the balanced noncentral-$t$ approximation for marginal $d=.30$ gives power .91738. At 20\% expected outcome loss, 640 randomised participants (160 per cell) give 512 expected observations and approximate power .92332. Under independent Bernoulli retention, the probability of at least 500 observed ratings for one endpoint is .89074 at $N=640$ and .99668 at $N=660$; these probabilities do not apply to joint availability or undefined outcomes. A local reproduction used 14 cells with 500 datasets each, including complete data at 500, MCAR/MAR/MNAR at 500 and 640, global null and joint alternative (.45 marginal authority and .60 interaction points). For 500 complete cases, H1 rejection was .900 and Holm-adjusted H3 rejection .624. At 640 with 20\% loss, H1 rejection was .922 under MCAR and .926 under MAR, but H3 rejection .606 and .550. Under MNAR at 640 the global-null H1 rejection rose to .596, and alternative H1 coverage fell to .376. The reference targets are estimated from large complete synthetic samples. These screening results do not validate nonadherence, unheard-version handling, bootstrap sensitivity or interaction power. Candidate recruitment of 640 or 660 remains conditional on feasibility and prespecified effects. The exact-source CI grid (run 45988878791 at \texttt{dbb0f17}) subsequently evaluated 76,000 datasets at 1,000 per cell. At 660 randomised, the joint alternative yielded H1 rejection .932 under MAR and .755 with 15\% assigned-feature suppression. Corresponding Holm-adjusted H3 rejection was .586 and .348. Under MNAR, global-null H1 rejection was .572 and alternative H1 coverage .412 without suppression. No fits failed. These findings support pilot evaluation of task fidelity, not a guarantee of adequate interaction power or a final sample-size choice. Subsequent exact-source bootstrap screening (run 45990267481 at \texttt{0180814}, four cells of 40 datasets, 199 draws per dataset) included 5--10\% non-hearing by execution and 15\% feature suppression. At $N=640/660$, heard-only IPW coverage under the authority null was .950/.975 under MAR and .500/.475 under MNAR. All 160 full-cohort numerical analyses were blocked by the undefined-outcome gate. Mean observed ownership ratings at 660 were 485.8 under MAR. Under independent measurement loss .20 and hearing loss .05/.10 within two balanced execution groups, the exact retention distribution is the sum of independent binomials with probabilities .76 and .72. For $N=660,700,720$, probabilities of at least 500 observed ratings are .16209, .94361 and .99736, respectively. At 720 the expected number is 532.8. The 720 candidate addresses retention only, not identifiability of the full-cohort outcome, joint endpoint availability or power under feature suppression. The simulated MAR/MNAR outcomes need not obey these independent-loss probabilities.

Store approved pseudonymous records outside Git; public code and synthetic data only. Freeze materials, randomisation, scoring, missingness and analysis before confirmation. \section{Discussion}
The factorial design separates selection authority from execution rather than comparing broad automation levels. Preference congruence may partly explain the authority contrast; execution may influence effort and attention. Neither mechanism is identified by the marginal assignment effect alone. A finite candidate bank and transparent rule improve experimental control but limit generalisation to open-ended commercial generation. Self-reported agency does not establish neural or therapeutic effects. The initial bibliography is targeted, not exhaustive.
