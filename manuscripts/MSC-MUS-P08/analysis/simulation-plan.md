# P08 initial factorial calibration v0.2.0

36 cells: randomised N=240/480/720, latent marginal authority contrast 0/.45, latent interaction 0/.60, MCAR/MAR/MNAR outcome loss (20% expected separately per outcome). Balanced four-cell assignments, random baseline covariates, shared participant variation, execution-dependent heteroskedasticity, three rounded bounded items per outcome. Agency has the same latent marginal authority contrast but no latent interaction. Complete-data reference contrasts are independently estimated from 40,000 synthetic participants for each effect combination. Reference estimates have finite uncertainty. Coverage concerns the observed-scale reference; latent null is not automatically an exact observed-scale null under rounding, ceiling effects or selection. H1 uses unadjusted alpha .05; H2/H3 use Holm. Report unadjusted marginal interval coverage, not simultaneous coverage. Unconditional rejection counts numerical failures as non-rejections. Report joint rejection, bias and Monte Carlo uncertainty. Fit calls the same production HC3 array estimator. This is a 500-replication screen (18,000 datasets), not a final sample-size calculation. Retained cells require at least 1000 replications and a justified smallest effect. Allocation is exactly balanced rather than a full simulation of variable-block stratification. The generator does not yet model partial tasks or nonadherence explicitly. Outcome availability is simulated at composite level, not item level. No empirical claims.

## Stage A extension

Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python simulation.py --reps 2000 --reference-n 200000 --nonadherence .15`. The seeds are 2808 and 2809 for the factorial and bootstrap calibrations, respectively. Factorial calibration ran on Python 3.12.14, NumPy 2.3.5 and SciPy 1.17.0. Every one of the 36 cells applies overlapping 15% probabilities of losing the assigned positive feature during execution. Model design retains the originally randomised cells. This is an illustrative suppression pattern, not a fitted nonadherence model. Conditional and unconditional rejection, coverage with MCSE, bias, RMSE, contrast-specific failures and complete-data reference SEs are in `calibration-stage-a.csv`. Report the classification as type-I rejection only for an exact population null; under MNAR it is rejection relative to that null in the presence of selection bias. Reference-sample SE for an exact-null row is the numerical fit SE on the reference sample, not uncertainty of the exact null.

There were no numerical failures in 72,000 datasets. At N=720 with both latent effects active and MAR, unconditional rejection was .781 (H1), .803 (H2), and .400 (H3). It was .078, .083 and .161 under the chosen MNAR scenario. Under the joint population null, biased complete-outcome selection in MNAR raised unconditional rejection across N to as much as .598 for H1 and .488 for H2; this shows the risk of selection, not a numerical failure. Zero is the exact full-data null for all three tests when both effects are absent. At 2,000 replications, the maximum binomial MCSE is .0112 and MCSE near 5% is .0049. The hypothetical alternatives are not pilot effects. None of these results supports a 90% primary planning target at or below N=720 for the active-effect stress case, nor does this determine whether an alternative design is feasible.

Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python bootstrap_calibration.py --reps 100 --draws 199 --jobs 4`. For N=240, known-heard selection depends on execution but not authority, and the ownership authority target is zero. Across 100 datasets per mechanism the cap-20 IPW and zero-shift intervals covered .93 and .94 under MAR (MCSE .026 and .024), and .76 and .77 under MNAR (MCSE .043 and .042). All datasets yielded reportable intervals. This limited calibration probes interval implementation under selected scenarios and demonstrates the impact of a false missingness assumption. It is not a final certification of the other 26 shifts, H2/H3 or the full design. Increase outer and bootstrap draws for narrower precision after pilot calibration.
